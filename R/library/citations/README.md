# R/library/citations: verified primary-source citations (issue #1)

Tooling that attributes the records of a compilation to the studies that
measured them and lets a new citation enter the bibliography only after
automated verification against Crossref and OpenAlex, or an explicit decision
by the owner. `R/RunMe.r` sources only the offline part (`citations_config.r`,
`normalise_citation.r`, `parse_reflists.r`, `build_bib.r`, `provenance.r`);
everything that talks to a service runs by hand through `run_citations.r`, and
the Sheet is read by `RunMe.r` only with `DataSheets = TRUE`, to refresh the
tracked copies it then reads (`R/library/sheet_snapshots.r`, issue #118). No
Google Scholar.

## Tracked data

- `sources/databases/<Src>/primary_references.csv` (or `primary_references_<Label>.csv` in a folder several labels share, such as `DataRetriever/primary_references_Brose_2005.csv`; the label's `reflist_specs` entry names the folder, the cached frame and the file): one row per native reference key a source's records cite (`raw_citation` verbatim and immutable; `parsed_*` query fields; the resolved `doi`, `bibcite`, `cite_id`; `match_status`, `match_reason`, `services`, the locally computed agreement, `verified_at`, `tool_version`, `verification_mode` (the mode of the last automated verification, `full` or `crossref_only`, section "Crossref-only mode" below); the owner's `decided_by` / `decided_at`). The owner may edit `role`, `parsed_*`, `notes`, `owner_review` and `parsed_url` only (`owner_review` holds the reason a reference must pass through the queue; `parsed_url` the web address of a DOI-less database-style reference; both, like `verification_mode`, are optional in older files and read as NA when absent).
- `Bib/source_provenance_classes.csv`: the registry of every `source_mass` label (`class`: primary, compilation, derived, database, live, unknown; `default_provenance_type`: the type a record gets when it carries no resolved primary reference; `equation_bibcite` for allometry-derived sources; `value_tier`, 1-3, the trust tier of the source's values for the record-level range rule of issue #34, `R/library/exclude_discordant.r`, owner-editable: 1 compiled or measured adult values, 2 maxima-based or allometric, 3 single specimens, individuals and web-level values; a converted value is tier 3 by its conversion CiteID, not by this column). RunMe.r stops on an unregistered label or a tier outside 1-3.
- `Bib/pending_citations.csv`: the review queue. Rows are appended by `--queue`, never rewritten; approval is a committed `decision` with `decided_by` and `decided_at`: `1|2|3` (that candidate; its `c<n>_services` must include `crossref`, since the entry is built from the Crossref record -- an OpenAlex-only candidate is rejected by `--apply-queue` with the proposal `doi:<DOI>` (re-verified at Crossref) or `nodoi`, #114 item 3; a decision applied before the rule is history and not re-checked), `doi:10.…` (re-verified), `manual:<Key>` (an entry the owner added to the curated bib), `nodoi` (a DOI-less entry built from the corrected `parsed_*`; `parsed_author1` may then hold the approved author list in BibTeX form, `Ikeda and Hirakawa and Imamura`), `self`, `drop` (clears the wrong candidate's `doi`, agreement flags, OpenAlex id and notices on the rejected row; `raw_doi` and `services` stay; `self` clears them too). A row whose DOI changes under a decision loses its `bibcite` / `cite_id` so that `--bib` mints or reuses a key for the new DOI. A `certain` row has no queue row of its own; the owner overrides it by adding a row for the key by hand with a `doi:` decision, which is re-verified at Crossref like any other and leaves the row `approved` / `owner_doi` (#114 item 5; the Gonzalez 2011 case was hand-edited before this path existed). A candidate or `doi:` decision may carry `:year=YYYY` (owner decision 2026-10-04): the bib entry and the Citation cell take that year instead of the service record's (a digitised thesis deposited with its scan date), the entry's `note` says so, and the override is kept in `primary_references.csv` (`year_override`); it belongs to the work, so `--bib` applies an override recorded on any accepted row of a DOI to the entry and the key year whichever row builds them (the first recorded override of a DOI wins; before 2026-10-08 the row that sorted first decided, and `Dunning:2008aa` had reverted to Crossref's 2007 when a Myhrvold_2015 row of the same DOI was approved without the override). An empty `decision` defers the case: it stays `pending` and is reported as such, not as an error. A key's current decision is the last decided row the queue holds for it (the queue is append-only: a fold or an override is a new row after the old one), and the earlier decided rows are history that `--apply-queue` never replays; the current decision is applied unless the row already carries it -- `decided_at` equal and the status, reason and DOI (or key) the decision produces, `DecisionOnRow()` -- or carries a newer owner decision (a row set by hand under a decision item, the Gavrilov `self` rows of Makarieva_2008). Before 2026-10-08 every decided row of a key was applied in queue order and `decided_at` alone said whether it was on the row, so a second `--apply-queue` on a re-decided key cleared its `bibcite` by replaying the old decision first (Jones_2009 2152, Nowak 1999) and a second decision taken on the same day as the first was never applied (Jones_2009 2655, the MOM fold). The recommendation block (`recommendation`, `recommendation_reason`, `recommended_by`, `owner_note`; added 2026-10-06, optional in files written before it and read as NA) sits between `scite_note` and `decision`: `--recommend` fills the first three for every open row under the rules of the section "Recommendations" below (`recommended_by` `policy:<rule>`), a session may set a row by hand (`agent`, kept until `--recommend --force`), and `owner_note` is what the owner typed beside a decision on the Sheet tab (section "Owner decisions in the Sheet"); `--apply-queue` copies a non-empty note into the row's `notes` as `owner <decided_at>: <note>`. None of the four is a decision. Every uncertain case is collected and put to the owner in one batch at the end of a round, never settled by a default. `--queue` never re-appends a key that has an open row or a recorded decision not yet applied to its row (the row's `decided_at` differs from the queue's; #114 item 4, reported as "not re-queued, their recorded decision awaits --apply-queue"); a key whose applied decision later left it pending again (a retraction) is a new case and is queued. `--dedupe-queue` (no `--source`) removes the duplicate rows the earlier behaviour left: an open row equal in every case column to an earlier row of the same key, open or decided (`open_duplicate`), and a decision recorded twice for one key with the same `decision`, `decided_by` and `decided_at` (`same_decision`); the first row of a key is always kept, and two decisions or an open row with new candidates are different cases and stay. The removed rows are printed and written to `reports/dedupe_queue_<date>.md`. Applied 2026-10-06: queue rows 161, 162 (Herberstein_etal_2022 h:3cfad141, h:fc0ce1f8, open re-appends of decided keys) and 332 (Lislevand_etal_2007 key 33, decision `1` recorded twice) removed, 1,275 -> 1,272 rows.
- `Bib/scite_checks.csv`: the record of the selective retraction / correction screen (the Screening section below), written in Claude Code sessions from the Scite MCP tools (`checked_by = scite-mcp`; `editorialNotices` read per DOI) or, when Scite is unavailable, returns nothing or fails on quota or permission, from the Consensus MCP tools (`checked_by = consensus-mcp`; Consensus has no retraction field, so the row says `notice_type = unchecked`); when neither answers the gap is recorded (`checked_by = none`). A row that reports a retraction or a correction turns a certain or approved row back to pending (`retracted`); a missing row, a `none` row or an `owner-waiver` row never changes a status. `services` of a screened DOI gains `;scite-mcp`, `;consensus-mcp` or `;owner-waiver` (never `;none`). Neither service ever writes a bib entry or a Sheet row.
- `Bib/TaxonBodyMass_PrimaryCitations.bib`: generated (`%% GENERATED … do not edit`), rebuilt by `--bib` from every source's accepted references and the response cache (`AssignPrimaryKeys()`), keys in byte order; a key present in the curated `Bib/TaxonBodyMass_Citations.bib` as well fails the pipeline. An accepted DOI with no Crossref record gets no entry and keeps its `bibcite` / `cite_id` as they were; `--bib` lists such rows in its report instead of stopping (#114 item 3). `--bib` writes back a source's `primary_references.csv` only when one of its values changes (`WritePrimaryReferencesIfChanged()`; the files written are named in the report): an optional column the file lacks or a quoting difference is no change, so `Leahy_2025/primary_references.csv` is no longer rewritten on every run; and a row that keeps its key keeps its `cite_id` rather than taking the id a Sheet row gives the same Bibcite or DOI (`KeepOrMintCiteID()`; the stale `DunningJr_2007` row once flipped Wilman's Dunning08 id) -- the table's id is consulted only for a row that has none (#114 item 7).
- `Bib/BM_primary_citations_snapshot.csv`, `Bib/BM_citations_snapshot.csv`: the two citation tabs of the Sheet as last read, by `--sheet` (both tabs, on a dry run too) or by `RunMe.r` with `DataSheets = TRUE` (which also writes `sources/BM_data_snapshot.csv`). Section 8 of RunMe.r reads these copies and never the tabs (issue #118), so they are the input of the pipeline as well as the record of every append; a round that appends to the Sheet commits the refreshed copies on its branch.
- `Bib/decision_items.csv`: the questions for the owner that are not a queue row (a role, a key rename, a year to confirm, a fold of two entries; `item_id` `<Src>-<n>`, `asked_at`, `source_label`, `question`, `options`, `recommendation`, `answer`, `answered_at`). The agents append items; `--decisions-push` puts them on the tab `BM_decision_items` (the same columns plus `status`, append-only on `item_id`); `--decisions-pull` copies a new `answer` and the pull date into the file, writes `pulled <date>` to the tab and `item_id: question -> answer` to the round log; a session then implements the answer by hand (there is no code path from an answer to a file). `Bib/BM_decision_items_snapshot.csv` is the tab as last read.
- `Bib/BM_decisions_snapshot.csv`: the tab `BM_decisions` as last read by `--decisions-push` (after the append; on a dry run as it is) or `--decisions-pull` (after the status column is written): the owner's decision cells, notes and the pull's statuses beside the key, the case and the recommendation (section "Owner decisions in the Sheet"). `RunMe.r` never reads it; it is the record of what the owner saw and typed.

## DOI-less entries: the conventions for the owner's `parsed_*` fields (#114, item 1)

A `nodoi` decision builds the entry from the row's `parsed_*` fields and nothing else (`BuildBibEntryNoDOI()`), so the owner writes them under a small convention; `NoDOIEntryType()` reads it. A DOI-less work two sources approve keeps one key (`MatchingNoDOIEntry()`: same first surname, year and normalised title) and its entry is built from the row decided first (`NoDOIEntryOwner()`, `decided_at` then source label and key; 2026-10-08 -- before that the twin's fields were used on the pass that keyed the new row and whichever keyed row sorted first on every later pass, so a second `--bib` silently rewrote shared entries such as Silva 1995 or Bonaccorso 1998 from the later-decided row). Nothing in `primary_references.csv` was changed for it: the files written before 2026-10-06 are read as they are, and the next `--bib` applies the types below to them (Speakman & Thomas 2003 and Keister & Buck 1964 become `@incollection`; Lockyer 1981, `Mammals in the Seas 3, 379-487`, stays `@article` until the container carries a marker).

| `parsed_*` | entry |
| --- | --- |
| `parsed_author1` `Surname`, or a BibTeX list `A and B and C`; a corporate author in braces, `{Birdcare Avicultural}` | the `author` field as written; the braced name becomes `author = {{Birdcare Avicultural}}` (one name for BibTeX, never escaped); the key and CiteID take its words (`Birdcare-Avicultural:2021aa`, `BirdcareAvicultural_2021`); the Sheet cell drops the braces |
| `parsed_container` `<degree> thesis, <school>` (or `<school>, <degree> thesis`) | `@mastersthesis` for an `M.S.`, `MSc`, `M.A.`, `MPhil` or `Master's` degree, `@phdthesis` for any other thesis or dissertation; `school` is the text after (else before) the thesis word |
| `parsed_container` `In: <Editors> (eds.), <Book title>. <Publisher>, <Place>` (form A; an optional pages clause may precede the `In:`, `pp. 84-116. In: ...`) or `<Book title> (<Editors>, eds), <Publisher>, <Place>` (form B, the form of the Speakman and Keister rows) | `@incollection` with `editor`, `booktitle` (form A: up to the first sentence end after the marker; form B: before the bracket), `publisher` (the rest), `volume` and `pages` as given; the editor marker is `(eds)`, `(Eds.)`, `(ed. Name)`, `(editor)`, `(editors)`, `, eds)`, or the word `editor(s)` / `eds.` outside brackets (then the sentence before it names the editors). A singular `ed.` outside brackets is an edition (`2nd ed.`), not a marker. A pages clause in the container (`pp. 84-116`, `p. 127-223`, the leading `84-116. In:`) fills `pages` when `parsed_pages` is empty. `In:` without a marker still gives `@incollection`, with the editors unknown |
| a database-style reference: a URL in `parsed_url`, in the container (`www.birdcare.com.au (accessed 24/08/2021)`), or -- when the container or the notes say database / web page / online / accessed -- in `notes`; no volume or pages | `@misc` with `howpublished` (the container) and `url` |
| otherwise | a volume or pages: `@article` with `journal`; a container alone: `@book` with `publisher`; a bare title: `@misc`. `parsed_url`, when set, adds a `url` field to any type |

## Hop 2: a reference that is itself a database source

`BuildProvenance()` recognises a resolved reference (`certain`, `approved`, `nodoi_approved`) that stands for another source label of the database: its `cite_id` is a registered label, or its `bibcite` or `doi` is that of the label's curated bib entry (`MatchIntermediateLabel()`; the DOI route needs the label's entry to carry a `doi` field, which `Chown:2007aa` gained for the Herberstein_etal_2022 pilot). Such a reference is an intermediate compilation, and the record is attributed through it (`IntermediateReferences()` reads the intermediate's own records of the same species and their accepted or self references):

| the intermediate's record of the species | row written | hop | `provenance_type` | `via_cite_id` | `primary_*` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| resolves to a primary reference (one row per reference) | through the intermediate | 2 | `compiled_via_compilation` | the intermediate's CiteID | the intermediate's reference (`match_status` that reference's) |
| is the intermediate's own measurement (role `self`) | the intermediate is the measurement | 1 | `compiled_from` | NA | the intermediate |
| none, keyless, pending, or the intermediate has no `primary_references.csv` yet | the intermediate is the terminal citation (owner decision 2026-10-04) | 1 | `compilation_terminal` | NA | the intermediate (`ref_role = compilation`) |

No third hop is attempted: a reference of the intermediate that is itself a label stays as that label. A record-level `prov_type` override is never changed. The terminal rows become hop-2 (or `compiled_from`) rows by themselves once the intermediate's reference list is ingested; nothing in the source's `primary_references.csv` changes, so `role` stays `measurement` there and the owner may set it to `compilation` if wanted (the hop-2 rule does not depend on it). Herberstein_etal_2022 -> Chown_etal_2007 is the first case: 244 ant records cite Chown et al. 2007, whose Appendix S1 holds the authors' own measurements of those eight species, so they will read `compiled_from` citing Chown once `Chown_etal_2007` is ingested (its S1 keys are `self`); until then they are `compilation_terminal` at Chown.

## Derived sources: the length sources and the equation

A source whose registry row is `class derived`, `default_provenance_type derived_allometry` with an `equation_bibcite` (Feldman_etal_2016, Meiri_2018) computes its masses from a length. A keyless record of such a source gets one row, `ref_role = equation`, citing the equation (the registry default). Where the parse script keeps the length sources as `ref_keys` (Meiri_2018, Stage 2), `BuildProvenance()` writes two kinds of rows per record: one per reference with `ref_role = measurement` and `provenance_type = derived_allometry` (the role's usual `compiled_from` is replaced for a derived source, so that the type says the value is computed; the primary citation is the length source's, `match_status` the reference's; an unmatched key stays `unknown` / `unmatched_key`), and one `ref_role = equation` row per species x label counting the records, citing the `equation_bibcite`. No record-level `prov_type` override is needed; a record that carries one is left to the override and gets no equation row. A length source that is itself a database label is not followed to a second hop under a derived source (the value is the computation, not the intermediate's record). The equation row is always resolved, so the per-source `pct_resolved` of a derived source counts it; the share of length-source links resolved is reported separately in the round's notes.

```

#   owner decides: on the Sheet tab BM_decisions (section "Owner decisions in the Sheet") or
#   by filling `decision` in Bib/pending_citations.csv and committing
Rscript R/library/citations/run_citations.r --source Kiorboe_2013 --apply-queue --bib
Rscript R/library/citations/run_citations.r --source Kiorboe_2013 --sheet               # dry run
Rscript R/library/citations/run_citations.r --source Kiorboe_2013 --sheet --no-dry-run
```

Every verifying step writes `reports/citations_<Src>.md`; `--screening-list` writes `reports/screening_<Src>.md` only. `--offline` forbids network access (cached responses only); `--force` re-verifies `certain` rows.

Service quotas. Crossref's polite pool is generous; OpenAlex allows 1,000 requests a day without an API key (since 2025; the `x-ratelimit-*` headers count them per day) and answers the rest with 429 and a Retry-After of up to a day. `CachedGET()` does not wait for that: the reference that hit the quota and every later one needing that service are left unverified (status NA, named in the report line `--verify: n reference(s) left unverified`), the decisions taken so far are written, and the next `--verify` (after the reset, or with `OPENALEX_API_KEY` set in the environment) finishes the rest from the cache plus the missing calls. Found on the Herberstein_etal_2022 pilot (2026-10-04), when two pilots ran on one day. A reference needs OpenAlex for the retraction flag of a DOI it carries and for the second service of an open search. A list too large for one day is verified in slices with `--verify --min-records N`: only the keys cited by N or more records are verified, the others stay unverified (status NA, counted in the report line `--min-records N: n key(s) ... left unverified`) until a later run without the option (owner's rule for Jones_2009, 2026-10-06: the 880 keys on two or more records first, the 2,186 singletons on later nights).

## Crossref-only mode (owner decision 2026-10-06: used for the backlog while the OpenAlex key's daily budget is ~1,000 lookups)

`run_citations.r --source <Src> --verify --crossref-only` (`VerifyPrimaryReferences(crossref_only = TRUE)`, `DecideMatchCrossrefOnly()`) decides a reference on Crossref alone. OpenAlex is not called at all (no work lookup, no title search; the ~6,300 waiting references would take a week of the key's daily budget), and Crossref `update-to` / `updated-by` notices and the screening file are read as always. A reference is `certain` with reason `crossref_only`, `services` `crossref` and `verification_mode` `crossref_only` when:

| rule | condition |
| --- | --- |
| (a) | the `raw_doi` given by the source resolves at Crossref and author and year agree, or `title_sim >= 0.80` (today's `doi_resolves` logic without OpenAlex) |
| (b) | the best Crossref candidate is strong -- `title_sim >= 0.93`, author, year within 1, and one of container / volume / pages -- what the two-service rules call `single_service`, there being one service (the `two_service_agreement` thresholds applied to Crossref alone) |
| (c) | a title-less citation ("Journal volume: pages (year)") and a Crossref candidate -- from the raw-string query or the container-filtered query, #128 -- whose container (abbreviation-aware), volume, first page and year (within 1) all agree and whose type is an article's (`titleless_candidate_types`) -- the `container_volume_page` rule on Crossref alone |
| (d) | a candidate from the compilation's deposited `reference[]` with `title_sim >= 0.95`: `certain` / `closed_world` as now, marked `crossref_only` |

Everything else is as in the two-service rules: a runner-up within 0.05 under another DOI (or two DOIs agreeing on all four fields of rule (c)) is `pending` / `ambiguous`; a retraction or correction notice `pending` / `retracted`; a disagreeing or unresolvable source DOI `pending` / `doi_mismatch`; grey literature, `weak_match`, `below_threshold` and `no_candidates` unchanged; `single_service` and `service_unavailable` never arise. The mode takes only the rows no two-service decision has reached -- unverified, `pending` / `service_unavailable`, `pending` / `single_service`; a row decided with OpenAlex, pending or certain, is not touched. `--force` re-decides every row the normal run would take, and the certain rows, in Crossref-only form (a `two_service_agreement` row becomes `crossref_only`). `--crossref-only` needs `--verify` or `--queue`.

Provenance and the later full check. `verification_mode` is an optional, tool-written column of `primary_references.csv`: `crossref_only`, `full` (Crossref + OpenAlex), or NA for a row verified before the column existed and for owner-decided and self rows; a file lacking it reads as NA and is not rewritten for the column alone. A later `--verify` without the flag (when OpenAlex budget exists) re-checks every certain `crossref_only` row in full (`RecheckCrossrefOnly()`): where OpenAlex agrees the row is upgraded to `two_service_agreement` / `doi_resolves` / `closed_world` with `verification_mode` `full`; a notice makes it `pending` / `retracted`; where OpenAlex returned the DOI too but the two-service rules end `pending` (an ambiguous twin) that decision stands; where OpenAlex disagrees -- it returned no row for the DOI but a plausible candidate (`title_sim >= 0.70`) under another -- the row is demoted to `pending` / `doi_mismatch` and queued with both services' candidates; where OpenAlex is silent (nothing, or nothing plausible) the Crossref-only acceptance stands and only `verified_at` is stamped, so that the next `--verify` re-checks it again from the cache. A row whose DOI changes under a new automated decision loses its `bibcite` / `cite_id` for `--bib` to re-mint, as under a queue decision. `reports/citations_<Src>.md` names the mode in its header (`Verification mode: crossref_only ...` or `full`), counts the source's certain rows that rest on Crossref alone, lists `services` and `verification_mode` per reference, and its `--verify` lines give the rows decided on Crossref alone and the outcome of every re-check (`upgraded to ...`, `pending/doi_mismatch`, `kept`). `reports/warnings_citations.md` counts the certain `crossref_only` rows apart: the Totals line "certain references resting on Crossref alone" and the `refs_crossref_only` column of the coverage table. The screening list (`--screening-list`) does not list such a row as a `disagreement` for lacking OpenAlex -- that is the mode, resolved by the full re-check -- and it enters the audit-sample pool like any other certain row. `crossref_only` is a reason code added to the issue's list for the mode.

## Title-less references: the two Crossref paths (issue #128)

A reference with no title and a whole journal key -- "Journal volume: pages (year)", the shape `ParseJournalOnlyStyle()` reads from the PHYLACINE `Mass.Source` cells (`TitlelessJournalKey()`: `parsed_title` empty, `parsed_container`, `parsed_volume` and `parsed_pages` present) -- cannot be found by a title and is decided by the `container_volume_page` rule alone. Crossref's bibliographic query on the raw string ranks the journal's front matter above the article: for 236 of the 471 such keys of the Faurby_etal_2018 round (2026-10-06) the top candidates were a 'NOTES', 'Author Index', 'Frontispiece' or 'Contents' item of the same volume and year, or the journal issues themselves, and for 'Global Ecology and Biogeography 18: 19-29 (2009)' (91 records) the issues only. `VerifyReference()` therefore searches such a reference at Crossref twice (2026-10-08): the raw string first, as for every reference, then `CrossrefQueryContainer()` -- `GET /works?query.container-title=<container>&query.bibliographic=<volume> <pages>&filter=from-pub-date:<year-1>,until-pub-date:<year+1>&rows=10` -- whose items are the works of that journal around that year that mention the volume and the pages. The pages go in as the source writes them ('19-29'), not the first page alone: Crossref indexes its page field as one token, and on eight Faurby keys with known answers the range found all seven that exist where the bare first page found five ('83 1' is lost among issue numbers). Without a year there is no date filter; without a container, volume or pages there is no request. The two candidate sets are pooled, one row per DOI, and decided as before: Crossref-only mode accepts on the four fields through rule (c), the two-service rules through `container_volume_page` once OpenAlex returns the DOI too (the top candidate is looked up there as for any reference). A candidate agreeing on all four fields is the work only when its type is an article's (`titleless_candidate_types`; `TitlelessAgreement()`): a journal's front matter deposited as a chapter or a paratext at the cited page is not, and an item without page numbers (de Gruyter's old Mammalia volumes) never agrees. Two agreeing DOIs (the Allen Press and OUP deposits of a Journal of Mammalogy article) are `pending` / `ambiguous` and recommended under `titleless_twin`. The OpenAlex counterpart (`filter=primary_location.source.display_name.search:<container>,biblio.volume:<volume>,biblio.first_page:<page>`) is not implemented: the backlog runs in Crossref-only mode, and the full re-check looks the accepted DOI up at OpenAlex directly. The recorded responses of the three keys above are the fixtures of `test_citations_services.R` and `test_citations_decide.R`. Applied on 2026-10-08 to every title-less key of Faurby_etal_2018 and Jones_2009 as an evaluation (`reports/container_query_Faurby_etal_2018.md`; the keys had been decided by the owner with `cite:` notes before the path existed, so nothing was rewritten), see `audit/provenance_rounds.md`.

## Decision rules (issue #1, section 2.3)

Service relevance scores are cached with the raw responses but never enter a decision. Candidates of type dataset, component, peer-review, journal issue and the like are dropped before scoring. For each reference:

| condition | status / reason |
| --- | --- |
| `raw_doi` resolves at Crossref and (author and year agree, or `title_sim >= 0.80`) | `certain` / `doi_resolves` |
| `raw_doi` present but unresolvable or disagreeing | `pending` / `doi_mismatch` |
| no candidate at all | `not_found` / `no_candidates` |
| best `title_sim < 0.70` and the citation reads as grey literature (thesis, report, unpublished, …) | `pending` / `grey_literature` |
| best `title_sim < 0.70` otherwise | `not_found` / `below_threshold` |
| no `parsed_title` (a title-less citation, "Journal volume: pages (year)"; `ParseJournalOnlyStyle()`) and a candidate whose container (abbreviation-aware, `ContainerMatch()`), volume, first page and year (within 1) all agree and whose type is an article's (`titleless_candidate_types`: Crossref `journal-article` / `proceedings-article`, OpenAlex `article` / `review` / `letter`, or unknown; `TitlelessAgreement()`, #128), returned by Crossref and OpenAlex (owner decision 2026-10-06); the Crossref candidates come from both the raw-string query and the container-filtered query (section "Title-less references" below) | `certain` / `container_volume_page`; one service only: `pending` / `single_service`; two such DOIs: `pending` / `ambiguous`; no agreeing candidate: `not_found` / `below_threshold` (or `pending` / `grey_literature`) -- the title rules below are not applied |
| a retraction (OpenAlex `is_retracted`, Crossref `update-to` / `updated-by`, a screening row) or any editorial notice on the best DOI | `pending` / `retracted` (never auto-certain); a missing or `none` screening row is no notice (selective policy, below) |
| runner-up with a different DOI within 0.05 of the best | `pending` / `ambiguous` |
| best not strong (`title_sim >= 0.93`, author, year within 1, and one of container / volume / pages) | `pending` / `weak_match` |
| strong and Crossref and OpenAlex both return that DOI | `certain` / `two_service_agreement` |
| strong, one service, the candidate comes from the compilation's deposited reference list, `title_sim >= 0.95` | `certain` / `closed_world` |
| strong, one open-search service | `pending` / `single_service` |
| the citation is the compiler's own study or unpublished data | `self`, no service call |
| the reference list (`review_col`) or the owner (`owner_review` in `primary_references.csv`) gives a reason to review the reference (a known alias, a thesis, a book chapter, a work without body sizes) and the services would accept it | `pending` / `owner_review`: queued with its candidates, never auto-certain (the standing rule that theses, books and near-misses always queue) |
| OpenAlex was known to be unavailable when the decision was made (`services` without it; an exhausted quota normally leaves the reference unverified instead, see Service quotas) and the decision would rest on the services' agreement or their joint retraction flags | `pending` / `service_unavailable`: the Crossref candidates are kept, the row is not queued for the owner, and the next `--verify` decides it; a disagreeing source DOI, grey literature, a notice and a self reference are decided as usual |
| Crossref-only mode (`--crossref-only`, section above): the source DOI resolves at Crossref with author + year or `title_sim >= 0.80`; or the best Crossref candidate is strong; or a title-less citation agrees on container, volume, first page and year | `certain` / `crossref_only`, `services` `crossref`, `verification_mode` `crossref_only` (a closed-world candidate `>= 0.95` stays `certain` / `closed_world`); the row is re-checked in full by the next `--verify` without the flag |
| Crossref-only mode: the full re-check of a certain `crossref_only` row finds OpenAlex disagreeing (no row for the DOI, a plausible candidate under another) | `pending` / `doi_mismatch`, queued with both services' candidates; OpenAlex agreeing upgrades the row, OpenAlex silent keeps it |

`weak_match`, `below_threshold`, `no_candidates`, `service_unavailable` and `container_volume_page` (title-less citations, 2026-10-06; such certain rows enter the audit sample of the screening list like any other) are reason codes added to the issue's list so that every queue row says why it is there (`service_unavailable` rows are not queued). `unscreened` (a `none` screening row forcing pending, 2026-10-04) stays in the vocabulary for the queue rows and files written before the selective policy; the tool no longer emits it. Requests time out after 60 s and a dropped connection is retried like a 5xx.

## Owner decisions in the Sheet (tab `BM_decisions`; owner decision 2026-10-06)

The owner decides the open queue rows on a tab of the lab Sheet instead of editing the CSV or answering a markdown batch in prose. `run_citations.r --decisions-push` (dry run; `--no-dry-run` writes) appends one row per open queue row to `BM_decisions` -- created with its headers when missing -- keyed on `source_label` + `native_key` + `queued_at` (a key re-queued after a pull is a new row; a row already on the tab is never rewritten, so a recommendation changed after the push is not shown: delete the tab row to push it again). Columns, in this order: `source_label`, `native_key`, `queued_at`, `n_records`, `reason`, `parsed` (the parsed fields as one line, `Author (Year) Title. Container vol: pages`), `c1`..`c3` (each candidate a clickable `HYPERLINK` to doi.org labelled `title (year) container [sim]`), `notice`, `fields_complete` (yes / no: whether a `nodoi` entry could be built), `recommendation`, `recommendation_reason`, `decision`, `owner_note` (free text, or `cite: <full citation>` to supply the fields of a DOI-less entry, step 4 below), `raw_citation`, `pushed_at`, `status`. After a real push the `decision` column gets a dropdown (`accept`, `1`, `2`, `3`, `nodoi`, `drop`, `self`, `defer`; not strict, free text allowed) through the Sheets API `batchUpdate` (`SetDecisionValidation()`; googlesheets4 exports no such call). If the request is rejected the push stands and the dropdown is set by hand once: select column N from row 2 down, Data > Data validation > Add rule > Dropdown, the eight values above, "Show a warning" (not "Reject the input"). One-time cosmetics for the owner: View > Freeze > 2 columns (A:B) and wrap the `parsed` column. Both tabs are snapshotted (`Bib/BM_decisions_snapshot.csv`, `Bib/BM_decision_items_snapshot.csv`).

What the owner does on the tab:

1. Open `BM_decisions`; sort or filter by `source_label` or `recommendation` (the tab may be sorted freely: the pull matches rows by key, not by position).
2. Type `accept` in the first `decision` cell and fill it down the column: every row then takes the recommendation shown beside it. A row whose `recommendation` is empty needs a typed decision (`accept` there is reported as an error and the row stays open).
3. Change the rows you disagree with: a candidate index (`1`, `2`, `3`), `nodoi` (a DOI-less entry from the `parsed_*` fields; complete them in `primary_references.csv` first when `fields_complete` says no), `drop`, `self`, or free text in the grammar of the queue -- `doi:10.1234/abc` (checked at Crossref by the pull), `manual:<Key>`, `1:year=1998` (a year override on a candidate or `doi:`).
4. Leave a cell empty or type `defer` to keep the row open for later; put anything you want recorded in `owner_note` (it goes into the row's `notes` at `--apply-queue` as `owner <date>: <note>`). For a DOI-less entry, type the full citation in `owner_note` as `cite: Smith, J. & Jones, A. (1987) Body size of shrews. Journal of Mammalogy 68: 123-130.` (the prefix in any case, with or without a space; owner request 2026-10-08): the pull parses it into the queue row's `parsed_*` fields (author, year, title, container, volume, pages), an empty `decision` cell then counts as `nodoi` (a typed `nodoi`, `accept`, `doi:` or candidate decision stands and still takes the fields), and `--apply-queue` copies the fields into `primary_references.csv` before building the entry, so `parsed_*` is never edited by hand for such a row. The citation is read in APA style, `Smith, J., & Jones, A. (1987). Body size of shrews. Journal of Mammalogy, 68(2), 123-130. https://...` (`ParseAPAStyle()`: a trailing URL is dropped, a journal item without a title, `Allgaier, A. (1993). Bat Research News, 34(4), 100.`, is accepted on journal, volume and page alone and the status says `; no title`, a thesis `(Doctoral dissertation, University of X)` and a chapter `In Editor (Ed.), Book (pp. 1-10). Publisher.` are read into the conventions of the DOI-less entries), as well as in the other styles the parser knows. The status shows what was read, `pulled <date> (cite: Smith (1987) Body size of shrews. Journal of Mammalogy 68: 123-130)`; a citation the parser cannot read gives `error: cite: could not be parsed (missing: <fields>); edit the note or add the fields in parentheses` and the row stays open with nothing written until the note is fixed. `--recite` (no `--source`) re-parses every `cite:` row of the queue, decided or not, with the current parser -- the notes pulled before `ParseAPAStyle()` existed were read by the generic path, which swallowed "vol(issue): pages. Publisher" into the container or found none -- writes the queue, prints the change table (rows re-parsed, cells changed per column) and `reports/recite_<date>.md`, and leaves alone, listed, any row whose note still lacks a required field; run it before `--apply-queue`, which copies the queue's fields into `primary_references.csv` only when it applies a decision.
5. Answer `BM_decision_items` (the questions that are not a queue row) in its `answer` column.
6. Tell the session "pull". Pull while you are not editing or sorting the tab: the `status` column is written back in the order the tab was read, and a sort between the read and the write would misplace it (the next pull re-derives every status from the queue).

`--decisions-pull` reads the tab, resolves every row (`ResolveTabDecision()`): an empty cell or `defer` -> `deferred`; `accept` -> the recommendation on the tab; a typed decision must match the grammar and pass the apply-time checks of `QueueDecisionProblems()` (a candidate the row has no DOI for, an OpenAlex-only candidate, a year override on `nodoi`), and a `doi:` must resolve at Crossref (with `--offline` it is pulled unchecked and said so; `--apply-queue` still stops on it). A sound decision is written into `Bib/pending_citations.csv` with `decided_by owner`, `decided_at` the pull date and the `owner_note` (with the `parsed_*` fields of a `cite:` note, parsed by `ParseCiteNote()` through `ParseCitationString()`; the parse check lives here, `QueueDecisionProblems()` is unchanged); a problem is written to the row's `status` as `error: <why>` and the row stays open -- nothing stops. A row already decided in the queue reads `pulled <date>` when the cell agrees and `ignored: already pulled ...` when it was changed afterwards (a change of mind after the pull is made in the CSV, or by a new queue row). A key twice on the tab (two pushes racing) and a tab row without a queue row are errors. The pull then writes the `status` column, snapshots the tab, prints `pulled n, deferred n, errors n, ignored n` per source, writes `reports/decisions_pull_<date>.md` and appends a dated entry to `audit/provenance_rounds.md`. The agents then commit the queue (`Bib: owner decisions pulled from BM_decisions (<date>)`) and run the existing per-source steps as before: `--apply-queue --bib`, `--sheet` dry run then real, the pipeline, one PR per source (small ones grouped), the round-log entry. A pulled decision is a committed decision like one typed into the CSV: `WritePendingQueue()`'s unapplied-decision rule holds the key back from re-queueing until it is applied.

## Recommendations for the open queue rows (`--recommend`; owner decision 2026-10-06)

`run_citations.r --source <Src> --recommend` (or `--recommend --all-sources`, every source, with the rule x source table in `reports/recommendations_<date>.md`) fills `recommendation`, `recommendation_reason` and `recommended_by` for every open row of the queue (`RecommendDecisions()`, `recommend.r`): the first matching rule of the table wins, nothing is asked of a service, and the same files give the same result. A policy row is recomputed at every run; a row set by a session (`recommended_by` `agent`) or the owner (`owner`) is kept unless `--force`. Thresholds in `citations_recommend`: a candidate is *strong* when its `title_sim >= 0.90` (`accept_sim`), its year is within 1 of the parsed year (`year_window`) and Crossref returned it (an OpenAlex-only candidate cannot be accepted by index, #114 item 3); *fields complete* means `parsed_author1`, `parsed_year`, `parsed_title` and `parsed_container` are all present (what a `nodoi` entry needs). The owner reads the recommendation on the Sheet tab and accepts or changes it; the recommendation itself is never applied.

| rule (`recommended_by` `policy:<rule>`) | condition | recommendation; reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `self_role` | the row's `role` in `primary_references.csv` is `self` | `self` |
| `owner_review_flagged` | the row carries an `owner_review` reason | empty; the reason text |
| `titleless_twin` | no `parsed_title`; container, volume and pages present; reason `ambiguous` (two DOIs agreed on journal, volume, first page and year, #128) and both candidates from Crossref | the non-JSTOR index when exactly one is a `10.2307/` DOI, else `1`; "two DOIs agree on journal, volume, page and year (c1 X, c2 Y): change to 2 if preferred" (the twin policies of 2026-10-06 applied to a title-less key: the Allen Press / OUP deposits of a Journal of Mammalogy article) |
| `titleless_journal_key` | no `parsed_title`; container, volume and pages present, otherwise | empty; "title-less journal key: no Crossref candidate agrees on journal, volume, page and year (raw-string and container-filtered queries, #128); cite: the full citation for nodoi, or drop" |
| `titleless_incomplete` | no `parsed_title` otherwise | `drop`; "no title and no journal key" |
| `correction_notice` | reason `retracted`; `scite_note` names a correction, erratum or corrigendum and no retraction; candidate 1 at `title_sim >= 0.95` (`correction_sim`), year within 1, from Crossref | `1`; "notice is a correction, not a retraction" |
| `retraction_notice` | reason `retracted` otherwise | empty; "retraction or unread notice" |
| `jstor_twin` | candidates 1 and 2 both strong, exactly one a `10.2307/` DOI | the non-JSTOR index; "publisher DOI over the JSTOR twin" (the Lislevand precedent) |
| `publisher_twin` | reason `ambiguous`; candidates 1 and 2 both strong, neither JSTOR | `1`; "two publisher DOIs (c1 X, c2 Y): change to 2 if preferred" (owner policy 2026-10-06: the top-scored one) |
| `strong_candidate` | candidate 1 strong | `1`; "title_sim S, year within 1" |
| `openalex_only_candidate` | candidate 1 reaches the similarity and year but Crossref did not return it | `nodoi` if fields complete, else `drop`; "OpenAlex-only candidate; `doi:<DOI>` if it resolves at Crossref" |
| `grey_complete` | reason `grey_literature`, fields complete | `nodoi` |
| `fields_complete_no_match` | fields complete | `nodoi`; "best candidate sim S is another work" (or "no candidate") |
| `fields_incomplete` | otherwise | `drop`; "missing: <fields>; complete parsed_* and decide nodoi to keep it" (owner policy 2026-10-06) |

## Screening: the selective policy (owner decision 2026-10-05)

What `certain` requires. A reference is `certain` when Crossref and OpenAlex agree on the DOI under the rules above and the Crossref `update-to` / `updated-by` and OpenAlex `is_retracted` fields of that DOI are clean; both fields are read at every `--verify` for every DOI. No row in `Bib/scite_checks.csv` is required, and a DOI with no row, or whose latest row is `none`, keeps whatever status the services and the owner gave it (`ApplySciteChecks()`, `SciteVerdict()`). Before 2026-10-05 a `none` row forced `pending` / `unscreened` and the Smith_2003 round needed an `owner-waiver` row per DOI to undo it; the earlier rule's `;none` suffix and `screening:none` note are removed from a row the next time the overlay runs.

When Scite is called. Scite (the Consensus MCP tools as its fallback, recorded as such) is used selectively, on the DOIs `ScreeningCandidates()` lists for a source -- `run_citations.r --source <Src> --screening-list`, printed and written to `reports/screening_<Src>.md`, no network:

| category | rows listed | reason codes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `notice` | a DOI whose services carry a retraction or correction notice (`match_reason` `retracted`, `is_retracted`, or an `editorial_notice`): Scite reads the notice type | `retracted` |
| `disagreement` | the two services disagree or only one answered: `single_service` and `ambiguous` rows, and any certain / approved / pending row whose `services` lacks Crossref or OpenAlex (a certain row accepted in Crossref-only mode excepted: it lacks OpenAlex by design, waits for the full re-check and enters the audit-sample pool) | `single_service`, `ambiguous` |
| `doubtful` | doubtful identities: grey literature (`grey_literature`, or the citation text matches `citations_grey_pattern`), a DOI shared by several keys of the source, a source DOI disagreeing with its record (`doi_mismatch`), a reference before `citations_screening$old_year` (1960) | `grey_literature`, `duplicate_doi`, `doi_mismatch`, `old_journal` |
| `audit_sample` | a random sample of the newly certain DOIs (certain rows with no `scite-mcp` or `consensus-mcp` row, not listed above): `sample_frac` of them (5%), at least `min_sample` (3), all when fewer | `audit_sample` |

The sample is drawn with `sample.int()` under the seed `strtoi(substr(sha1("<Src> <date>"), 1, 7), 16)` (`ScreeningSeed()`; the date is the day of the draw, printed with the seed in the report), so that a round's draw can be repeated; the session's random state is restored afterwards. One row per native key, in category order, the reasons joined; rows without a DOI, `self` and `rejected` rows are never listed; a DOI already screened by `scite-mcp` is listed only under `notice` (Consensus cannot read a notice type, so a `consensus-mcp` row does not close a notice case). The agent screens the listed DOIs, appends one row per DOI to `Bib/scite_checks.csv` (`scite-mcp`, or `consensus-mcp` with `unchecked`, or `none`), and the next `--verify` applies the overlay: a reported retraction or correction turns a certain or approved row to `pending` / `retracted` and queues it; a clean row only joins `services`. The round log (`audit/provenance_rounds.md`) records the list's counts, the seed and the outcome; nothing else changes in `Bib/source_provenance_classes.csv` or the round-log conventions.

The `owner-waiver` row (`checked_by = owner-waiver`, `notice_type = waived`; owner decision 2026-10-05 for the JSTOR-era Mammalian Species accounts of Smith_2003) predates the policy and is no longer needed: `ReadSciteChecks()` still accepts it, `SciteVerdict()` still treats it as a screen without notice superseded by any later `scite-mcp` or `consensus-mcp` row, and the 30 rows stay in the file for history. `--dedupe-queue` also tidies `Bib/scite_checks.csv` (`DedupeSciteChecks()`, rows removed by line so that every other line keeps its bytes): exact duplicates, an older row of the same DOI and service (the latest by `checked_at`, then position, is kept) and a `none` gap row for a DOI another row answers (`gap_answered`; a lone `none` row stays as the record of the gap). Applied 2026-10-06: the 30 `none` rows the Smith_2003 round recorded before its 30 `owner-waiver` rows for the same DOIs were removed, 671 -> 641 rows; no verdict changes (`SciteVerdict()` already ignored a `none` row beside an answering one).

## Hallucination-robustness rules (issue #1, section 6)

Nothing typed by a person or an LLM reaches a bib file or the Sheet: entries come from `BuildBibEntry()` (a Crossref record) or `BuildBibEntryNoDOI()` (owner-approved fields, with a note saying so); the Sheet's Citation cell from `FormatCitationText()`. `raw_citation` is immutable. Every accepted row stores the services, the similarity and agreement flags, `verified_at` and `tool_version`; every decision stores `decided_by` / `decided_at`. Keys and CiteIDs are reused by DOI before a new one is minted, collisions get suffixes. Sheet writes are append-only, dry-run by default, deduplicated on Bibcite, snapshotted, and go to `BM_primary_citations` only -- and, since 2026-10-06, to the two decision tabs `BM_decisions` and `BM_decision_items`, which hold no citation text that enters a bib or a Citation cell: the pull reads back what the owner typed as a decision string validated by the queue's grammar, never as a reference.

Key minting (#114 item 8, new entries only). A new key or CiteID takes the first author's surname as the record gives it, repaired token by token by `NormaliseSurname()` (an all-capitals token and a run of capitals after a lowercase letter are title-cased: `Evans-WHITE` -> `Evans-White`, `VillEGER` -> `Villeger`; `McLaughlin`, `DeLong`, `O'Gorman` stay) and folded to ASCII; a letter the record itself dropped (`Bmstedt` for Båmstedt, `Sma` for Srna, `Schnheit`) cannot be restored by code and stays the owner's to rename. The year is `KeyYear()`: the owner's `year_override` when recorded, else the citation's `parsed_year` when the record carries it among its dates (a paper online in 2012 and in print in 2013, cited as 2013, is keyed `:2013aa` / `_2013`, not Crossref's online-first `issued` year), else the record's year; the entry's `year` field stays the record's unless overridden. A row that already has a `bibcite` keeps it whatever these rules would give, and keeps its `cite_id` (item 7): the tool never renames an existing key (owner decision; `Bmstedt:1985aa`, `Evans-WHITE:2005aa`, `VillEGER:2012aa`, `Ikeda:2012aa` stay until the owner renames them in the Sheet), which `test_citations_bib.R` proves by keying a DOI under its record-spelt key beside a new row and by checking that every accepted tracked row carries a key.
