# Horn & de la Vega (2016) body composition of Wadden Sea birds and harbour seals

Source: Horn, S., & de la Vega, C. (2016) Relationships between fresh weight, dry weight, ash free dry weight, carbon and nitrogen content for selected vertebrates. Journal of Experimental Marine Biology and Ecology 481:41-48. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jembe.2016.04.010 (closed access; the abstract gives the carbon : fresh weight ratios, 0.16-0.22 for the birds and 0.17 for the seals). Bib key `Horn:2016aa` (`Bib/TaxonBodyMass_Citations.bib`), CiteID `Horn_2016`.
Data: the authors' PANGAEA deposits in supplement to the article, licence CC BY 3.0, downloaded 2026-10-05 as tab-delimited text (`?format=textfile`): `PANGAEA_894380_birds.tab` (https://doi.org/10.1594/PANGAEA.894380; 17 birds of 6 species collected 2002-2014 in the Schleswig-Holstein Wadden Sea: whole-bird wet mass, three subsamples each with wet, dry, ash and ash-free dry mass, and %C and %N of dry mass) and `PANGAEA_894400_seals.tab` (https://doi.org/10.1594/PANGAEA.894400; tissue samples of three harbour seals, *Phoca vitulina*: wet, dry, ash and ash-free dry mass and %C, %N per tissue type, with the animal's wet mass; no whole-body dry mass).

`summarise_pangaea.py` computes per bird DW/WW (sum of the three subsample dry masses over the sum of their wet masses; the paper fits the relationships as linear through the origin), AFDW/DW, C/WW (C fraction of dry mass x DW/WW) and N/WW, averages them within species and writes `horn2016_species_ratios.csv` (one row per species and a `BIRDS_MEDIAN` row): *Anas penelope* 0.3827 (2 birds), *Calidris canutus* 0.3937 (3), *Chroicocephalus ridibundus* 0.3876 (3), *Haematopus ostralegus* 0.4340 (3), *Larus canus* 0.3805 (3), *Limosa lapponica* 0.4279 (3); species-level medians DW/WW 0.3906, AFDW/DW 0.8527, C/WW 0.1677 (the pooled through-origin slope over the 51 subsamples is DW/WW 0.4033). These medians supply the `bird` row of `R/library/mass_conversion.r`. The seal tissue ratios (tissue-pooled DW/WW 0.29-0.34 per animal) are printed for the record only: no source converts a seal and the deposit holds no whole-body value.

StoichLife (`Gonzalez_2025`) holds the same 17 birds (14 at species level): its `body.weight.gr` is the whole-bird wet mass x the bird's own DW/WW rounded to two decimals (*Limosa lapponica* 299.2 g x 0.43 = 128.656 g), so the `bird` factor restores the measured wet masses to within the rounding.

Not a body-mass source: no BodyMass_*.r script reads this folder.
