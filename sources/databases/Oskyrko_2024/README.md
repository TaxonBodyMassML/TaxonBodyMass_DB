# ReptTraits (Oskyrko et al. 2024)

Source: Oskyrko, O., Mi, C., Meiri, S., & Du, W. (2024) ReptTraits: a comprehensive dataset of ecological traits in reptiles. Scientific Data 11:243. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-03079-5 (CC BY 4.0; Europe PMC PMC10899194). Bib key `Oskyrko:2024aa` (`Citation.bib`).
Data: figshare 10.6084/m9.figshare.24572683 (CC BY 4.0; record version 4 of 2024-04-02 holds `ReptTraits dataset v1-1.xlsx`, the version the paper describes, and `ReptTraits dataset v1-2.xlsx`, each one workbook with four sheets); the folder holds the four sheets of v1.2 exported to csv by M. Novak (2026-10-04): `ReptTraits dataset v1-2_data.csv` (12,060 species x 40 traits plus taxonomy, 3.8 MB), `ReptTraits dataset v1-2_sources.csv` (the same table with the citation of every value in place of the value, 6.3 MB), `ReptTraits dataset v1-2_references.csv` (the reference list, 1,306 entries under the headings Online databases, Published databases, Other papers, 231 KB) and `ReptTraits dataset v1-2_traitdefinitions.csv` (6 KB). Taxonomy follows the Reptile Database (Uetz 2023); 1,288 sources, 1820-2023 (the paper's counts). Body mass is trait T20, `Maximum body mass (g)`, "the maximum body mass of grams (without sex)" (trait definitions); the paper's Methods say of it that "most mass data are based on lengths, transformed to masses based on taxon-specific equations (accounting for the degree of limb loss in relevant lineages)". The two files name one species differently (`Phelsuma v-nigra` in the sources file, `Phelsuma vnigra` in the data file); the parser aligns them.

Columns used: `Species`, `Maximum body mass (g)` (data file) and the same cell of the sources file (kept as `ref_keys`), `Order`, `Family` (taxonomy hints; `class` set to Reptilia).
Filters: rows with a positive numeric mass (10,469 of 12,060 species: Squamata 10,131, Testudines 313, Crocodilia 24, Rhynchocephalia 1). Of these the length-derived values are dropped (see Imputed rows); 56 rows (56 species) are kept: 40 squamates, 15 turtles, 1 crocodilian.
Mass type: wet mass in grams, the species maximum (the DB's other reptile sources Feldman_etal_2016 and Meiri_2018 are maxima too, computed from the maximum SVL); no conversion.
Imputed rows: the source carries no imputation flag, but its body-mass column is, by the authors' own statement and by its values, mostly computed from length. Three `DropImputed()` steps (logged to audit/imputed_rows.csv): (1) 9,765 rows whose citation is a length-mass allometry compilation -- Slavenko et al. 2019 (6,237 rows) and Zimin et al. 2022 (2,734), Meiri-lab macroecological analyses whose squamate masses are the Feldman et al. (2016) equations applied to maximum SVL; Meiri et al. 2021 (324 rows, nearly all turtles and crocodilians), whose Methods say "for reptiles, however, mass data are rarely reported. Therefore ... we converted lengths to masses using the allometric equations published by Feldman et al." and whose masses "are maxima for ectotherms"; and the equations' own papers Feldman & Meiri 2013, Feldman et al. 2015 and Feldman 2015 (the thesis). (2) 619 rows whose value is identical (1e-6 log10) to the Feldman_etal_2016 or Meiri_2018 value of the species although cited to a field guide, a longevity compilation or a primary paper (the citation is the source of the length, not of a weighed animal; the parser reads the two source tables as their parsers do). (3) 29 rows within 0.0105 log10 of that value (the estimate re-rounded to one decimal). Over the raw file, 5,957 of the 10,469 masses are identical to the Feldman_etal_2016 or Meiri_2018 value and 8,937 (85%) are within 0.0105 log10 of it; 9,179 values carry exactly one decimal, as Feldman's column does. The 56 kept rows are the ones no evidence identifies as estimates; whether they are measured is not established (several carry equation-like values: 681.3 g for both Gonyosoma margaritatum and Phrynonax sexcarinatus, 1,202 g for both Kinixys nogueyi and K. zombensis; O'Shea 2018, cited on 19 of them, is a field guide that gives lengths), and the owner decides on them (issue #77). Lepidosaurs, turtles and crocodilians only; no fossil taxa in the file.
Source label: `Oskyrko_2024`.

## Record set

56 records, one per species (`n = 1`), 56 taxa, all binomials (no genus-only rows, no subspecies, no placeholders); masses 0.7 g (Letheobia zenkeri) to 417,000 g (Chelonoidis niger). Shared species with the other reptile sources after the drops: Feldman_etal_2016 3 (none identical: Anolis campbelli 3.0 vs 2.9 g, Parastenophis betsileanus 265.5 vs 308.3 g, Philodryas boliviana 1,245.6 vs 66.3 g), Meiri_2018 1, Meiri_2024 0, AnAge 1 (Alligator mississippiensis 62,000 vs 150,000 g), Amniote (Myhrvold_2015) 0. The dependency registry (`Bib/source_dependencies.csv`) records `Oskyrko_2024 -> Feldman_etal_2016` (copies; over the raw file shared 8,851, identical 5,953 (67%), within 0.0105 8,103 (92%)) and `Oskyrko_2024 -> Meiri_2018` (derived_same_input; shared 6,164, identical 34, within 0.0105 5,516 (89%)); both document the removed copies and collapse nothing in the kept frame. The 197 raw rows cited to De Magalhaes & Costa 2009 (AnAge) share no value with the AnAge frame (173 of them are Feldman values), so no AnAge edge is registered; the 39 rows cited to Carey & Judge 2000 are likewise Feldman values (33 identical).

## Provenance

Compilation (`Bib/source_provenance_classes.csv`: class `compilation`, default type `unknown`). Record-level, hop 1: the parser keeps the citation cell of every kept row as `ref_keys` (author-year keys as written in the sources file, comma-separated, several per row allowed; `SplitRefKeys(adat$mass_src, ',')`): 56 of 56 kept records keyed, 22 distinct keys. `references.csv` (`key`, `citation`) maps 19 of them to the verbatim entry of `ReptTraits dataset v1-2_references.csv`; the key is written as in the sources file (so `Seymor 1987`, `Franca et al. 2008`, `van Leenders 2019` for "Leenders, T. ... (2019)"), the entry is kept verbatim with its typographic errors ("Iverson, J. B., Christine P. B., Kathy K. B. and Lyddan, K. K."). Three keys were matched by the species' family where the list holds two entries of that author and year: `Kraus 2017a` -> the Gerrhopilidae paper (Gerrhopilus addisoni), `Shine et al. 2006` -> the 2006a Atractaspididae paper (Xenocalamus michelli), `Wallach & Gemel 2018` -> the Herpetozoa 2018 Letheobia synopsis (Letheobia weidholzi, L. zenkeri; the list's other Wallach & Gemel entry is 2019). Three keys have no entry in the list and are reported as unmatched: `Aresco 2009` (Pseudemys gorzugi), `Wilson and Heinsohn 2007` and `Wilson et al. 2006b` (both Morenia ocellata). Reference list -> `primary_references.csv` by `run_citations.r --init --verify --queue` (round of 2026-10-04, see `audit/provenance_rounds.md`).
