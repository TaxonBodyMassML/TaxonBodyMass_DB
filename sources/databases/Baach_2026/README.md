# Baach & Dormann (2026) multitrophic dataset: individual masses of birds, mammals and invertebrates

Source: Baach, E. & Dormann, C. F. (2026) Multitrophic dataset for multi-taxon abundance and richness estimations from biomass and nutrient energetics. Scientific Data 13:1132. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-07997-4 (open access, CC BY 4.0). Bib key `Baach:2026aa` (`Citation.bib`).
Data: the compilation is deposited on Zenodo (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18630029; three spreadsheets, also as .ods and per-sheet .csv). The sheet `individual mass` of the *Abundance Richness* spreadsheet and that spreadsheet's `References` sheet were exported by M. Novak as `individual mass.csv` (Windows-1252, 158 kB, 2,027 lines of which 935 are blank trailing rows; the e-ogonek of the reference key 'Jędrzejewska' became '?' in this code page) and `Abundance Richness - References.csv` (UTF-16, 11 kB, 22 entries: name in sheet, link/access/details, other links, notes), dated 2026-07-24 and 2026-05-19. The two exports are committed here: the Zenodo API refused the request from this machine, so the deposit's licence statement was not read; the article is published under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence, confirmed by the owner on 2026-10-05, and the deposit is treated the same way. `references.csv` (below) is a transcription of the `References` sheet. The sheet's columns: `name` (scientific name or the smallest taxonomic classification the source gave), `Family`, `tax_cat` (bird, mammal, invertebrate), `trophic_level`, `trophic_niche`, `indiv_mass` ("all mass converted to g and as text to protect values from conversion with download", note of the first row), `ref` (the compilation's source key), `alt refs`, `notes`. 1,092 data rows: 622 insect rows citing Chown et al. (2007) (the individual masses of its Appendices S1-S2), 144 vertebrate rows citing Jedrzejewska & Jedrzejewski (1998), 108 citing EltonTraits, 80 bird rows citing 'AVONET; Handbuch der Vogel Mitteleuropas' with the Handbuch volume and page, 77 wolf-spider and cricket rows citing Rendon et al. (2019), 29 small-mammal rows citing Golley, Petrusewicz & Ryszkowski (2009; the IBP synthesis volume, whose primary studies stand in `alt refs`: Buckner 1964, Hawkins & Jewell 1962, ...), 22 order- and family-level arthropod rows citing Norberg (1978), 9 land-snail rows citing Baba (2000) (`alt refs` there are the taxonomic authorities) and 1 Sus scrofa row citing Snow et al. (2025). The paper describes the dataset as "the mass of an individual of a given organism in grams" and says nothing about fresh or dry mass; the bird and mammal values are the handbook species means of their sources (AVONET, EltonTraits, the Handbuch, the two books), the Chown and Rendon rows individual measurements.

Columns used: `name`, `indiv_mass` (g), `ref` (kept as `ref_keys`), `tax_cat` (class hint: bird -> Aves, mammal -> Mammalia), `Family` (family hint where the value ends in -idae; the sheet also writes orders, a class and a subfamily there). `trophic_level`, `trophic_niche`, `alt refs` and `notes` are not used.
Filters: blank rows removed; every remaining row has a positive mass. Names are taken as written; the raw-name rules of pipeline step 2 remove the `Genus sp.` placeholders (`Teleogryllus sp.`, 52 rows; 47 insect `sp.` rows of the Chown set, `Melyrid sp.` and `Meloid sp.` among them), fold the two trinomials (`Locusta migratoria migratorioides`, `Apis mellifera ligustica`), and the bare genus `Tasmanicosa` (25 wolf-spider individuals, Rendon et al. 2019) enters the genus-only path of step 6 while the order- and family-level names (`Braconidae`, `Chalcidoidea`, `Lepismatidae`, `Miridae`, ...) leave there.
Mass type: grams as the compilation reports them ("all mass converted to g"); no conversion applied. The compilation does not state fresh or dry mass; its vertebrate sources report fresh mass and Chown et al. (2007) live mass, so the kept records are treated as fresh mass. The nine snail values of Baba (2000) are of unverified type (shell included or not), and the cited paper (Malakologiai Tajekoztato 18: 91-98, a loess-assemblage survey) holds no body masses and lists only one of the nine species, so their provenance is in question (see Provenance).
Imputed rows: none flagged in source. Two exclusions through `DropImputed()` (logged to audit/imputed_rows.csv): the Sus scrofa row (58,650 g; notes 'average from range'; Snow et al. 2025 visually estimated wild-pig masses) as the midpoint of a published range rather than a measurement, and the 22 Norberg (1978) rows, order- and family-level records (Araneae 18, Psocoptera 2, Curculionidae 2) of an energy-content study whose mass type the sheet does not state (unverifiable units; the names would leave at step 6 in any case). 1,069 rows kept (636 distinct raw names).
Source label: `Baach_2026`.
Provenance: compilation (`Bib/source_provenance_classes.csv`, default unknown); record-level, hop 1 through the `ref` key of every row (9 distinct keys, 2 on the AVONET rows), resolved in `references.csv` (columns `key`, `name_in_sheet`, `citation`, `doi`, `link`, `notes`: the References sheet's 22 entries; `key` is the sheet's name transliterated to ASCII, `citation` the sheet's citation text verbatim -- the export dropped the commas of the author lists -- `doi` the DOI of its doi.org link, `link` its URL or ISBN; the parser maps the `ref` strings to these keys with an explicit lookup and stops on an unknown string, and strips the Handbuch volume/page locator, e.g. '(B9 S605)', which stays in the raw file). Three of the keys are sources of this database and are registered as copies in `Bib/source_dependencies.csv`: `Chown_S.L. et al. 2007` -> `Chown_etal_2007` (622 rows; species shared 258, identical 258; 446 rows row-identical), `EltonTraits` -> `Wilman_etal_2014` (108 rows; 104 species shared, 104 identical, on the frame with the EltonTraits mammals of issue #65) and `AVONET; Handbuch der Vogel Mitteleuropas` -> `Tobias_2022` (80 rows, the AVONET value rounded to one decimal; 79 species shared, 79 within 0.0105), so those rows read through the intermediate compilation (hop 2, `compiled_via_compilation` / `compilation_terminal`) once the curated bib entries `Wilman:2014aa` and `Tobias:2022aa` carry their DOIs (`Chown:2007aa` already does). The `alt refs` of the Golley et al. rows (the IBP volume's primary studies) are not resolved: the compilation gives no reference list for them. Round of 2026-10-04 (tbmcite 0.1.0; `--init --verify --queue`, Phase C not run): 4 references certain (AVONET, EltonTraits and Rendon et al. 2019 by the sheet's DOIs, Chown et al. 2007 by Crossref + OpenAlex agreement), 2 pending (the Handbuch, a book without a Crossref DOI, matched only to its reviews; Jedrzejewska & Jedrzejewski 1998, whose Springer book DOI 10.1007/978-3-662-35364-6 resolves at title_sim 0.61), 2 not found (Baba 2000; Golley et al. 2009, the 1975 IBP 5 volume reissued, no Crossref DOI); Owner decisions (2026-10-04): Jedrzejewska & Jedrzejewski -> candidate 1 (the book DOI, authors taken into `parsed_*`); the Handbuch -> `nodoi` (Glutz von Blotzheim & Bauer 1997, from the stated fields); Golley et al. -> `nodoi` with `parsed_year` 1975 (the IBP volume's first edition; the 2009 reissue the compilation cites is noted); Baba 2000 -> `drop` (the linked paper holds no body masses; the nine snail records keep the compilation as their citation).
