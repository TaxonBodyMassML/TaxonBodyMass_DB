# Leahy et al. (2025) metabolic traits of Australian ants: individual worker body masses

Source: Leahy, L., Chown, S. L., Riskas, H. L., Wright, I. J., Carlesso, A. G., Hammer, I. J., Sanders, N. J., Bishop, T. R., Parr, C. L., & Gibb, H. (2025) Metabolic traits are shaped by phylogenetic conservatism and environment, not just body size. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 122(29): e2501541122. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2501541122 (open access, CC BY-NC-ND; Europe PMC PMC12305036). Bib key `Leahy:2025aa` (`Citation.bib`; the `doi` field was added on ingestion from the Europe PMC record).
Data: Dryad 10.5061/dryad.d7wm37qb7 (published 2025-07-30; Dryad deposits carry the repository's CC0 terms, the licence is not restated on the dataset page), files `mr_ant_525.csv` (2,805 rows x 43 columns, 1.0 MB), `metadata_mr_ant.csv` (column descriptions and units) and the deposit's README (kept here as `dryad_README.md`); downloaded by M. Novak 2026-10-04. The deposit's other files (two phylogenies, two R scripts, the climate-modelling zip) are not kept. One row per individual ant: 2,805 workers of 214 colonies, 114 names of 34 genera and seven subfamilies, collected alive at six locations along a 1,500 km climatic and soil-phosphorus gradient in south-eastern Australia (Glen Echo, Ku-ring-gai, Little Desert, Nangak Tamboree, Round Hill, Wilson's Promontory; `location`, `site.pcat`, `lat`, `long`), 10 or more individuals per colony from 1-3 colonies per species and site (Methods). Adult workers only: the paper tests "worker ants from 114 species" and the file has no caste column.
Licence: CC0 1.0 (README: Dryad repository terms, not restated on the record; https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.d7wm37qb7). The data file, the metadata csv and the Dryad README are tracked; the reference tables are ours.

Columns used: `species` (the name as written, 'Genus epithet'), `wet.mass` (individual wet mass, mg), `individ.code` (the ant's identifier, for the duplicate check), `subfamily`/`genus` for the inventory only; taxonomy hints class Insecta, order Hymenoptera, family Formicidae are set by the parser. Not used: `dry.mass` (see Mass type), the respirometry, activity, ventilation, climate and soil columns.
Filters: species-level binomials only. The 57 names that are not identified to species leave with their 1,245 rows: 10 'nr.' near-identifications (234 rows, e.g. 'Camponotus nr. nigriceps', 'Stigmacros reticulata (nr.)'), 45 'sp.' names and lettered or coded morphospecies with a species-group annotation (988 rows, e.g. 'Nylanderia sp. A', 'Camponotus sp. cla1 (claripes gp.)', 'Meranoplus sp. D1 (Group D)') and 2 coded names without 'sp.' ('Crematogaster lae1/lae3 (laeviceps gp.)', 23 rows); they are dropped by the parser's binomial pattern (`^[A-Z][a-z]+ [a-z]+$`, the Chown_etal_2007 rule) before `FixFormatting()`, so no vocabulary row is needed for 'gp.' or the bracketed 'nr.'. A row without a positive numeric `wet.mass` would be dropped, but none occurs. 'Camponotus consobrinusus' is the file's spelling (the file also has 'Camponotus nr. consobrinus'); the name is passed as written and resolved by the enrichment step.
Mass type: wet (fresh) body mass of the individual worker, mg -> g (/ 1000), no other conversion: "ants were instantly placed in a freezer at -20 C and wet mass was measured the following day" (Methods), i.e. after the metabolic assay. `dry.mass` (48 h at 50 C) is not used; the metadata say that a few individuals whose dry mass was lost in drying carry their colony's average dry mass, an imputation that does not touch `wet.mass`. Seven rows have dry.mass > wet.mass (one Pheidole megacephala worker at 0.105 mg wet / 0.546 mg dry, two Ochetellus clarithorax within 3% of equality, four of unidentified names); their wet masses sit within their species' ranges and are kept. One row per individual (`n = 1`), 5-108 individuals per species from one to three sites, so the species value is the geometric mean over individuals of Pass 1 in RunMe.r. Species geometric means run from 0.106 mg (Ochetellus clarithorax, Pheidole megacephala) to 76.7 mg (Myrmecia tarsata).
Source label: `Leahy_2025`.
Imputed rows: none flagged in source for wet mass (the colony-average imputation of `dry.mass` concerns a column the parser does not read). One individual (`individ.code` 4123, Crematogaster laeviceps, colony WS2) is listed on four rows with identical masses that differ only in the activity and baseline-noise columns of the trace; the three duplicates are dropped through `DropImputed()` and the individual kept once (logged to audit/imputed_rows.csv; 2,802 rows kept, 1,557 of them binomials).
Record set: 1,557 records of 57 species (34 genera, 7 subfamilies): Dolichoderinae 18 species (Anonychomyrma, Dolichoderus, Froggattella, Iridomyrmex 10, Leptomyrmex, Ochetellus, Technomyrmex), Dorylinae 1 (Zasphinctus steinheili), Ectatomminae 5 (Rhytidoponera), Formicinae 15 (Camponotus 7, Melophorus, Polyrhachis 4, Prolasius 2, Stigmacros), Myrmeciinae 8 (Myrmecia), Myrmicinae 9 (Aphaenogaster, Chelaner 2, Crematogaster 2, Meranoplus, Pheidole, Podomyrma, Tetramorium), Ponerinae 1 (Mesoponera australis). Four species are already in the database through Herberstein_etal_2022 (Myrmecia nigriceps, M. nigrocincta, M. pyriformis, M. tarsata); the other 53 species and most of the 34 genera are new.
Provenance: primary (`Bib/source_provenance_classes.csv`: class `primary`, default type `measured_in_source`). The body masses are the authors' own measurements; the deposit cites no source for them (its only references, Economo et al. 2018 for the phylogeny and Zomer et al. 2022 for the aridity classes, concern other variables). Every record therefore carries the constant key `self` as `ref_keys`, resolved in `references.csv` (columns `key`, `citation`; one entry, the paper itself, written with the words 'This study' so that `InitPrimaryReferences()` gives it role `self`, status `self`, never sent to a service) -> `primary_references.csv` (round of 2026-10-04, tbmcite 0.1.0: 1 self reference, 0 queued, 0 verified against a service). The self row is what lets a compilation that cites this paper (Herberstein_etal_2022 or a successor) be attributed `compiled_from` at hop 2 instead of `compilation_terminal`. No dependency edge: no other source in the database copies these 2025 measurements; the four Myrmecia species shared with Herberstein_etal_2022 come from other studies there and differ in value (M. nigriceps 61.5 vs 62.0 mg, M. nigrocincta 20.3 vs 16.4 mg, M. tarsata 76.7 vs 59.4 mg).
