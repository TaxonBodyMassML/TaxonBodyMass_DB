# DeLong et al. (2010) metabolic-rate and r_max compilations: body masses of Datasets S1 and S2

Source: DeLong, J. P., Okie, J. G., Moses, M. E., Sibly, R. M., & Brown, J. H. (2010) Shifts in metabolic scaling, production, and efficiency across major evolutionary transitions of life. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 107:12941-12945. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1007783107
Data: the two SI tables, `sd01.doc` (Supplemental Data 1, metabolic rates: for each of 1A Prokaryotes, 1B Protists and 1C Metazoans a table of endogenous and of active rates with the columns Species | Mass (g) | Metabolic rate (W); 165 prokaryote, 103 protist and 86 metazoan rows) and `sd02.doc` (Supplemental Data 2, r_max: for each of 2A Prokaryotes, 2B Protists and 2C Metazoans the columns Species | Body mass (g) | rmax (day-1); 37 prokaryote, 122 protist and 16 metazoan rows), transcribed to `sd01.csv` (`group, rate_type, species, mass_g, metabolic_rate_W`, 354 rows) and `sd02.csv` (`group, species, body_mass_g, rmax_per_day`, 175 rows). In the repository since 2026-08-20. The Methods state that "all original metabolic rate units were converted to W, and volumes and masses were converted to grams", that the source studies measured body mass by "weighing single individuals, and, for unicells, weighing large numbers of cells and dividing by the estimated number of cells and estimating volume from external dimensions", that protist masses missing from the source studies were taken from Fenchel & Finlay (1983), and that the r_max data set holds 37, 122 and 16 species or strains of prokaryotes, protists and metazoans. The authors averaged multiple values per species within each of their new data sets, but 32 prokaryotes, 10 protists and Daphnia magna appear in both tables, and 21 prokaryotes, 17 protists and Daphnia magna in both rate columns of sd01, so one of the 436 distinct names can hold up to five rows here (three for Paraphysomonas imperforata in sd02 alone), which the pipeline averages within the source.

Columns used: `species` (as `taxon`), `mass_g` (sd01) and `body_mass_g` (sd02); the table (`dataset`: sd01 or sd02) and `group` are carried in the cached frame for provenance. `rate_type`, `metabolic_rate_W` and `rmax_per_day` are not used.
Filters: rows with a missing or non-positive mass are dropped (none in the current files; 529 rows read, 528 kept). The 16 sd02 metazoan rows are rescaled from micrograms to grams (see Mass type) and the sd01 active-rate row of 'Entamoeba hystolitica' is excluded (see Imputed rows). A plausibility guard in the script stops the parse if a fish exceeds 1e6 g, any metazoan 2e8 g, any protist 1e-2 g or any prokaryote 1e-9 g (the tables' largest values are a 15 kg cod, a 700 g lobster, a 2.2e-4 g Noctiluca and a 3.6e-11 g Amoebobacter), so that a repeat of the unit error cannot reach the database silently. Names are taken as written; the pipeline removes the non-taxon labels ('Brachyuran larvae (megalops)', 'Hymenostome ciliate', 'Scuticociliate', 'UnID chrysomonad', 'UNID kinetoplastid', 'Unidentified amoeba', `R/library/fix_nontaxa.r`) and the 'sp.' rows, truncates strain and stage qualifiers to binomials ('Mycoplasma pulmonis UAB CTIP', 'Paraphysomonas imperforata (arctic)*', and the five Calanus pacificus stage rows N1 to F, which therefore feed one species mean) and corrects 'Tetryhymena pyriformis' and 'Sagitta elegana' (`fix_misspellings.r`); 'Pleuroxus denticulatis' resolves to Picripleuroxus denticulatus and 'Entamoeba hystolitica' to Entamoeba histolytica through GBIF.
Mass type: wet (fresh) mass in grams for every row, as the SI column headers state ('Mass (g)', 'Body mass (g)') and the Methods confirm, with one correction (issue #13, audit of 2026-10-03 below): the 16 metazoan rows of sd02, all from Savage et al. (2004), are in micrograms, not grams, and are multiplied by 1e-6 in the script. Evidence: every one of the 11 species with other sources in this database sits 10^4.6 to 10^7.1 above them (median 10^5.4; Gadus morhua 1.50e10 against 6 g to 88 kg in twelve other sources), Daphnia magna is 8.85e2 in sd02 against 9.39e-4 g in sd01 of the same paper (a factor 9.4e5), and all 16 rows are plausible after the correction (cod 15 kg, American plaice 277 g, dace 13.8 g, gudgeon 9.2 g, bleak 6.9 g, fathead minnow 1.8 g, darters 1.2 and 0.54 g, Daphnia magna 0.89 mg, Eurycercus 70 ug, Ceriodaphnia 34 ug, Chydorus 30 ug, Pleuroxus 23 ug, Filinia 0.25 ug) and impossible before. Savage et al. (2004, Fig. 4 caption) express their r_max body masses as fresh weight in mg, so the sd02 column holds Savage's mg values multiplied by 10^3 instead of 10^-3; the corrected values are therefore fresh (wet) masses. The unicell masses are cell wet masses from weighed suspensions or from cell volume (DeLong et al. Methods; the Rose & Caron 2007 protist data are tabulated by cell volume, which DeLong et al. converted to grams), consistent with the other unicell sources of this database (median log10 ratio 0.00). Caveat: the sd01 masses are those of the individuals whose metabolic rate was measured, which for some species are larvae or juveniles (Pagrus major 0.02 g, postlarvae and juveniles; Solea senegalensis 70 g, juveniles; the Calanus pacificus nauplius and copepodite stages) and for unicells a culture rather than a maximum size; they are kept as the source reports them.
Source label: DeLong_etal_2010 for all rows (the unit correction appends no conversion reference).
Imputed rows: none flagged in source. One row is excluded with `DropImputed()` (logged to `audit/imputed_rows.csv`): the sd01 active-rate row of 'Entamoeba hystolitica' (8.6e-6 g), 1e3 above the endogenous-rate row of the same species in the same table (8.6e-9 g, the Makarieva 2008 value and the mass of a 20-30 um cell); same mantissa, so a unit slip in one row; the endogenous row is kept.
Provenance: the SI gives sources per group, not per row, so no record can be attributed to a primary reference individually except where a group rests on a single compilation (for issue #1). sd01: prokaryote endogenous (121 rows) and active (44) rates from the compilations of Makarieva et al. (2005, Proc. R. Soc. B 272:2219-2224) and Makarieva et al. (2008, PNAS 105:16994-16999; the SI lists no prokaryote sources, the Methods cite both); protist endogenous rates (52) from Makarieva et al. (2008); protist active rates (51) from the authors' own survey, 44 references listed in `sd01.doc` under 'Sources for active protist rates' without row links; metazoan endogenous rates (15) from the zooplankton data of Gillooly et al. (2001, Science 293:2248-2251); metazoan active rates (71) from the authors' survey, 33 references listed under 'Sources for active metazoan rates' without row links. sd02: prokaryotes (37) from the authors' survey, 36 references listed under 'Sources for Prokaryotes' without row links; protists (122) from Rose & Caron (2007, Limnol. Oceanogr. 52:886-895); metazoans (16) from Savage et al. (2004, Am. Nat. 163:429-441), whose zooplankton masses partly come from Gillooly (2000, J. Plankton Res. 22:241-251) and whose fish r_max values come from field studies. The Makarieva 2008 prokaryote and protist rows and the Gillooly 2001 metazoan rows duplicate values held in this database under Makarieva_2008 and the Gillooly-derived sources (cross-reference issue #5).
