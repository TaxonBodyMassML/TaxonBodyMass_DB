# Smith et al. (2003) Body mass of late Quaternary mammals (MOM), lab version v10.2

Source: Smith, F. A., Lyons, S. K., Ernest, S. K. M., Jones, K. E., Kaufman, D. M., Dayan, T., Marquet, P. A., Brown, J. H., & Haskell, J. P. (2003) Body mass of late Quaternary mammals. Ecology 84:3403. https://doi.org/10.1890/02-9003 (Ecological Archives E084-094; the published data set is MOM v3.3).
Data: `MOM v10.2.xlsx`, an unpublished Smith-lab update of the MOM data set; who supplied it, when and under what terms is not recorded here (issue #4, open). Four sheets: `MOM v10.0` (the data, 5,734 rows x 25 named columns: `Index`, `Continent`, `Species Distribution`, `Order`, `FAMILY`, `Genus`, `Species`, `synonyms`, `Common`, `LogMass (g)`, `Combined.Mass (g)`, `Mass Reference`, `Mass Status`, `Status`, time of extinction, last occurrence, IUCN status and year, trophic fields, habitat mode, `MOMv5 vs. MSW3`, `LAB.COMMENTS.2016`, `Taxonomy Ref`, `TO.DO`), `Deleted species` (82 rows, "Species removed from MOM and why"), `METADATA` (144 rows) and `REFERENCES` (286 numbered entries under the heading "Literature added"). `MOMv10.2.csv` is a CSV export of the data sheet without the `Status` column; the pipeline does not read it (its fate is part of #4).
Licence: unpublished (README; https://github.com/TaxonBodyMassML/TaxonBodyMass_DB/issues/4). `MOM v10.2.xlsx` and its export `MOMv10.2.csv` are an unpublished Smith-lab update whose terms are not recorded; kept tracked while F. Smith's answer is pending (owner decision 2026-10-06) and not covered by the repository licence. `text_keys.csv` and the reference tables are ours. See sources/LICENSES.md.

The later entries carry taxonomy fixes and a Late Pleistocene North America revision (2014-07-07, "Updated extinct list for North America based on new literature reviews").

Columns used: `Genus` + `Species`, `Combined.Mass (g)`, `Status`, `Order`, `FAMILY`, `Mass Reference` (Provenance below).
Filters: only rows whose `Status` is extant, introduction or introduced are kept (the 249 extinct and 96 historical rows, MOM's Late Quaternary and historically extinct species, are excluded so that they do not enter the database; `sources/extinct_taxa/build_extinct_taxa.r` reads them separately); rows whose mass is -999 or empty are dropped. 4,599 rows for 3,776 raw names remain; a species has one row per continent it occurs on (bats one row in all) and the pipeline's first pass averages them.
Mass type: wet mass in grams, the species value per continent averaged over sexes and localities (METADATA "Body mass algorithm"). One value per row (`n = 1`).
Source label: 'Smith_2003'.
Imputed rows: none flagged; the workbook's `Mass Status` column marks 32 rows 'update' and 3,901 'valid' (not used).
Provenance: compilation (registry `Bib/source_provenance_classes.csv`: class `compilation`, default type `unknown`); record-level, hop 1 through the `Mass Reference` column, kept as `ref_keys` by the parser and resolved in `references.csv` (columns `key`, `citation`, `note`; 329 rows, written by `build_references.r`: the 286 numbered entries of the `REFERENCES` sheet verbatim, blank runs collapsed and the one two-line entry, 196, joined, plus 43 alias keys for the text and URL cells described below) for the citation tooling of issue #1 (`reflist_specs$Smith_2003`; `folder = 'Smith_etal_2003'`, `frame = 'Smith_2003'`).
- Key rule (`BodyMass_Smith_etal_2003.r`): a `Mass Reference` cell holds one entry number ('260'; 4,050 kept rows), several joined by an underscore and a blank ('60_ 129', up to five; 405 rows), a text alias, a URL or a mix ('60_ walkers online'; 113 rows), or '-999' for no reference (31 rows). The cell is split at '_' followed by whitespace (`SplitRefKeys(x, '_[[:space:]]+')`; the underscores inside URLs such as 'Dama_mesopotamica.html' are not followed by a blank, so they survive); integer tokens are keys as they stand; every text token, lower-cased with blank runs squeezed, is looked up in `text_keys.csv` (`text`, `key`, `note`), which maps it either to the numbered entry it names (`smithsonian book of NA mammals` -> 161 Wilson & Ruff 1999; the two IIASA data-set URLs that are entries 174 and 196; the bare `animaldiversity.ummz.umich.edu` -> 73 Myers 1995) or to an alias key with its own `references.csv` row (ten text keys: `WalkersOnline` 15 rows, `Walkers` 4, `DamuthWG` 11, `Goheen` 11, `MorganErnest` 4, `Woodman` 3, `PanTHERIA` 3, `MammSpp249` 1, `MammSpp459` 1, `AfghanPDF` 3; two further IIASA data sets `IiasaTropMoist` 3 and `IiasaAfrRainfor` 1; 31 web pages `web.<site>.<page>`, 14 of them species pages of the shrew site members.vienna.at/shrew), or to no key (the fragments 'pers com.' left by splitting 'Neal Woodman_ pers com.'); the alias row's citation is the longest verbatim spelling found in the sheet and its note comes from `text_keys.csv`. A text token the table lacks stops the run. Result: 4,568 of 4,599 kept rows keyed (99.3%), 429 rows with several keys, 256 distinct keys (213 numbers, 43 aliases); 31 rows unkeyed because the cell reads -999 (24 species with no keyed row, 7 with a keyed row on another continent); two numbers are not entries of the list and are kept as they are for the owner (`60130`, 6 rows, plausibly '60_ 130' run together; `655`, 1 row); 75 of the 286 entries are cited by no kept record (the Pleistocene literature 1-55 except 11, 13, 15 and 25, and 30 others).
