# Brown, Hall & Sibly (2018) equal fitness paradigm: mortality and energy-content tables

Source: Brown, J. H., Hall, C. A. S., & Sibly, R. M. (2018) Equal fitness paradigm explained by a trade-off between generation time and energy production rate. Nature Ecology & Evolution 2:262-268. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-017-0430-1
Data: Nature Ecology & Evolution Supplementary Information: `41559_2017_430_MOESM3_ESM.csv` (Supplementary Table 1: natural mortality rate, generation time and body dry mass, 2,041 records of 1,292 names; this is the data set of McCoy, M. W., & Gillooly, J. F. (2008) Predicting natural mortality rates of plants and animals. Ecology Letters 11:710-716. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1461-0248.2008.01190.x) and `41559_2017_430_MOESM2_ESM.csv` (Supplementary Table 2: body mass and ash-free energy content of 74 taxa). In the repository since 2023-01-12 (FracFeed_DB).

Columns used: Table 1 `Genus` + `Species`, `Dry mass g`; Table 2 `Taxon2` (binomial after the colon), `Body mass g`.
Filters: rows with missing or non-positive mass are dropped (none in the current files). Names are taken as written; the pipeline resolves or drops the common-name and genus-level entries of Table 2.
Mass type: Table 1 is dry mass that the source derived from McCoy & Gillooly's wet masses with a fixed ratio, "dry mass = (wet mass)/4" (Brown et al. 2018, Methods). Wet mass is recovered with that same ratio (dry / 0.25), with no external conversion reference. The inversion is exact for birds and mammals (other sources in this database / Table 1 dry values: 3.999 for birds, 4.000 for mammals; Accipiter gentilis 256.1 g x 4 = 1,024.4 g, the mean of the male and female masses in Dunning 2008) and within 0.015 log10 of the Brey (2010) fish factor for fish; the table maximum 3.75e7 g x 4 = 1.5e8 g is McCoy & Gillooly's largest value. For the invertebrates of Table 1 the comparison with other sources is inconsistent, so their converted values are kept under review (issue #7). Table 2 `Body mass g` is wet mass (Mus musculus 40 g, Passer domesticus 22 g) and is used unconverted.
Source label: 'Brown_etal_2018' (no conversion reference appended, as for Cohen_2014 and Mulder_2011, which also invert the source's own ratio).
