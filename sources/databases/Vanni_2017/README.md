# Vanni et al. (2017): body masses of the aquatic animals of the global nutrient-excretion database

Source: Vanni, M. J., McIntyre, P. B., Allen, D., Arnott, D. L., Benstead, J. P., Berg, D. J., Brabrand, Å., Brosse, S., Bukaveckas, P. A., Caliman, A., ... & Zimmer, K. D. (2017) A global database of nitrogen and phosphorus excretion rates of aquatic animals. Ecology 98:1475. https://doi.org/10.1002/ecy.1792 (Ecological Archives data paper; 74 authors, the two first authors M. J. Vanni and P. B. McIntyre compiled the data). Bib key `Vanni:2017aa` (`Citation.bib`, the owner's BibDesk entry; the copy in `Bib/TaxonBodyMass_Citations.bib` carries the Crossref DOI, journal, volume and pages). Source label `Vanni_2017` (issue #99).

## Data

Wiley Supporting Information of the data paper, downloaded by M. Novak 2026-10-05: `Aquatic_animal_excretion_data.csv` (10,534 records x 35 columns, 3.4 MB; classic-Mac CR line endings and a few non-ASCII bytes of mixed encoding), `Aquatic_animal_excretion_variable_descriptions.csv` (the 35 variables with units and ranges) and `ecy1792-sup-0002-metadatas1.pdf` (Metadata S1, 29 pages: the data paper's text, the metadata in the Michener et al. 1997 format and the reference list, pp. 15-25 'Data source references'). Licence: Metadata S1 Class III B.3 says "Users are free to use and analyze the data. We request that attribution is given to this presentation of the data, and that any changes to the dataset are detailed"; the article record at Crossref carries Wiley's standard terms (`onlinelibrary.wiley.com/termsAndConditions#vor`, no Creative Commons licence), so the two csv files (the data, under the authors' statement) are committed and the publisher PDF is kept locally and not committed (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ecy.1792, Supporting Information, `ecy1792-sup-0002-MetadataS1.pdf`; its reference list is transcribed into `references.csv` by `build_references.py`).

One record per excretion measurement of a field-caught animal incubated alone (the smallest animals in groups of similar size, the rate and mass per individual), from 92 sources (`Source number`; `Source name` is an author-year key, an 'unpubl' label, or 'Ikeda database', 90 distinct strings: three unpublished Vanni datasets share the name 'Vanni, MJ unpubl'); 10,534 observations of 491 taxa (`Species code`), the taxon named as the study named it (`Species name`: a binomial, 'Genus sp.', a family, a stage, a pooled group), with `Phylum`, `Class`, `Order`, `Family`, `Trophic guild`, `Ecosystem Type` (freshwater lentic / lotic, marine), `Energy Source`, `Habitat` (benthic / pelagic), `Resident ecosystem`, `Temperature`, the N and P excretion rates, incubation time, body C/N/P and `Data source` ('Raw data from author(s)' 9,104 rows, 'digitized from paper' 1,412, 'Supplemental material in paper' 18) and `Data provider(s)`. Body size is one column, `Dry mass (g)` (with its log10), on every record, 5e-8 to 670 g: "Vertebrates generally were weighed live to determine their wet mass (WM), and in some cases dry mass (DM) was also provided. Most invertebrates were euthanized and dried before weighing for dry mass (DM) ... In cases where DM was not measured directly, we estimated it by multiplying WM by a conversion factor of 0.25 (i.e. DM = 0.25WM) ... For mollusks and turtles, mass measurements represent only soft tissue" (Metadata S1, Class II B.3.a). There is no sex, life-stage, age or sample-size column.

Columns used: `Species name` (the binomial), `Dry mass (g)`, `Source name` (kept as `ref_keys`), `Class` (filters, conversion groups), `Ecosystem Type` and `Habitat` (conversion groups), `Phylum`, `Order`, `Family` (taxonomy hints). The excretion rates, body stoichiometry and environmental columns are not used.

## Record set

Filters: every row has a positive dry mass. The taxon name is cleaned in the parser along the rules of `audit/raw_name_patterns.csv` (a bracket group the pipeline has no rule for would stop the run): a bracket group after the genus or the binomial is removed and the first-written name kept ('Cosmocalanus (Undinula) darwinii', 'Hypsophrys (Neetroplus) nematopus', 'Atherinella hubbsi (Melanirus hubbsi)', 'Pseudocaranx dentex (aka Longirostrum delicatissimus)', 'Daphnia middendorffiana ("cladoceran" treatment)'); 'Lucilus (Notropis) cornutus' is the common shiner *Luxilus cornutus*; a lowercase third token folds into the species ('Dreissena rostriformis bugensis', 'Lampsilis radiata siliquoidea', 'Salmo trutta fario'); the two salp generations 'Salpa fusiformis-Blastozoid' / '-Oozoid' fold into the species (both adult forms, as Kiorboe_2013's 'Salpa maxima. Agg') and 'Euphausia superba gravid female' loses its condition mark; 'Notemigonus crysoleucasæ' loses a stray byte. Then only the rows named with a clean binomial are used (7,868 rows of 319 names after the clean-ups and the stage exclusion; the 'Genus sp.', family, order and pooled-group names such as 'Chironomidae', 'Zooplankton-mixed species', 'Palaemonetes pugio and vulgaris', 'Haemulon flavolineatum & H. plumieri' leave; 'Dytiscidae adult' and 'Zaitzevia adult' are not species either). No fossil taxa. The exclusions below (stage-named rows, Insecta, Amphibia) remove 733 further rows. 7,340 records of 300 species from 73 `Source name` keys remain: 3,642 ray-finned fishes (137 species from 33 keys: Small et al. 2011 558 rows of 12 species, Villeger et al. 2012b 509 of 24, Higgins et al. 2006 306 of *Dorosoma cepedianum*, Wilson & Xenopoulos 2010 251 of 14, Schaus et al. 2010 229, McIntyre et al. 2008 165 of 31, Torres & Vanni 2007 160 of 7, Vanni et al. 2002 196 of 20, ...), 1,300 rows of the Ikeda database (101 pelagic species: 42 copepods and other maxillopods, 42 malacostracans, 8 chaetognaths, 7 salps, 3 hydromedusae, 2 pteropods, the ctenophore *Beroe cucumis*, the appendicularian *Oikopleura dioica*, *Clione limacina*, ...), 1,161 bivalves (22 species: *Dreissena* of 7 studies, unionids, *Mytilus*, *Perna*, *Corbicula*, *Donax*, *Mercenaria*, *Crassostrea*), 424 gastropods (22 species: *Tarebia granifera*, *Potamopyrgus antipodarum*, *Nacella concinna*, *Crepidula fornicata*, *Bellamya chinensis*, ...), 759 malacostracans (42 species: euphausiids, mysids, amphipods, atyid and palaemonid shrimps, crayfish), 640 maxillopods, 172 cladocerans (*Bythotrephes longimanus*, *Daphnia*), 35 ostracods, 74 annelids, 83 chaetognaths, 256 gelatinous zooplankters, 2 cephalopods and 92 turtles (4 species of Sterrett et al. 2015). One row per incubated animal (`n = 1`), so the species value is the geometric mean over the records of Pass 1 in RunMe.r; the animals are those an excretion study caught and incubated, so a species value here is a field mean of the sizes studied, not a handbook adult mass.

Fish-size evidence (the question put to the owner in issue #99, decided 2026-10-05: the fishes are included, option a): species x study geometric means (dry mass back-converted to wet, see Mass type) against the database's current species values (`TaxonBodyMass.csv` of main adbc37a): median log10(database / Vanni) over 60 fish pairs 0.81 (quartiles 0.15-1.37), by study 0.3-1.9 (McIntyre et al. 2008 1.52 over 8 pairs, Villeger et al. 2012b 1.78 over 6, Haertel-Borer et al. 2004 1.62 over 4, Sereda et al. 2008 1.52, Small et al. 2011 0.56 over 3, Vanni et al. 2002 0.36 over 6, Wilson & Xenopoulos 2010 0.53 over 8, McIntyre 2006 -0.44 over 8; the one Ikeda-database fish, *Ranzania laevis* at 1.7 mg dry, 6.3 log10 below), amphibians 1.6-1.9 (2 pairs), whereas copepods (31 pairs, median -0.03), malacostracans (23, -0.01), turtles (3, 0.01), cladocerans, ostracods and gastropods (6, 0.17) agree with the database. StoichLife's Vanni-database fish rows, dropped on 2026-10-04 for that reason, are verbatim copies of these records (`Gonzalez_2025`, below); the fishes are kept here as the field means they are (owner decision 2026-10-05), so that a fish species value here is the mean of the sizes the excretion studies incubated, and the register of `audit/flagged_species.csv` is the place for the conflicts the range filter reports.

Mass type: body DRY mass in grams (`Dry mass (g)`; whole body for most taxa, soft tissue for molluscs and turtles), converted to wet grams with `ToWetMass(from = 'dry')` and the group factors of `R/library/mass_conversion.r`, the group chosen from `Class` and `Ecosystem Type`: `vertebrate` (0.25) for Osteichthyes, Amphibia and Reptilia, the inverse of the compilers' own factor DM = 0.25 WM, so that a vertebrate weighed live re-enters at its measured wet mass (a generic factor without conversion CiteID: the turtles keep the bare label; Gonzalez_2025 used the Brey 2010 fish factor 0.2415 for its fishes, 0.015 log10 apart); `crustacean_zooplankton` (Kiørboe 2013) for Maxillopoda, Branchiopoda, Ostracoda and the marine Malacostraca (euphausiids, mysids, hyperiids, penaeids of the Ikeda database, salt-marsh shrimps); `invertebrate` (Brey 2010) for the freshwater Malacostraca (atyid shrimps, crayfish, *Gammarus*); `mollusc` (Brey 2010) for Bivalvia, Gastropoda and Cephalopoda, the bivalves and the benthic gastropods scaled to whole wet mass including the shell with `whole = TRUE` and the Brey 2010 shell ratios (`bivalve` 0.44, `gastropod` 0.40; 1,585 rows), the pelagic pteropods of the Ikeda database (`Habitat` Pelagic: *Clio*, *Cleodora*, *Euclio*, *Limacina*, the naked *Clione*) treated as whole animals, as in `Ikeda_2014` and `Gonzalez_2025`; `annelid` (Brey 2010) for Polychaeta and Clitellata; `chaetognath` (Brey 2010); `gelatinous_zooplankton` (Kiørboe 2013, Lucas 2011) for Thaliacea, Appendicularia, Scyphozoa, Hydrozoa, Tentaculata and Nuda; `insect` (Studier & Sevick 1992) and `helminth` are assigned but their classes leave (Imputed rows; no species-level Turbellaria). The turtles' soft-tissue dry mass is converted without a shell adjustment (no shell ratio for Testudines in `mass_conversion.r`), as Gonzalez_2025 converts the same Sterrett et al. 2015 records; their values agree with the database's (*Chrysemys picta* 287 g vs 292, *Sternotherus odoratus* 135 vs 146, *S. minor* 199 vs 170). Values with `source_mass` `Vanni_2017; Brey_2010` (1,997 rows), `Vanni_2017; Kiorboe_2013` (1,353), `Vanni_2017; Kiorboe_2013; Lucas_2011` (256) and bare `Vanni_2017` (3,734: the fishes and the turtles, generic factor).

Imputed rows: none flagged in source (every record is a measurement of an incubated animal, the dry mass measured or derived from the measured wet mass by the compilers' stated factor). Three exclusions are made through `DropImputed()` (logged to `audit/imputed_rows.csv`), in this order: (1) 205 rows (21 names) whose taxon names a larval or juvenile stage, before the binomial filter: the calyptopis / furcilia stages of *Euphausia superba* ('C3', 'C3-F2', 'F1-F2', 'F1-F3', 'F4-F5'), 'Euphausia pacifica late Fursilia', 'Furcilia larva (Euphausia superba)', 'Megalopa larva A/B/C', 'Alima larva', 'Decapod larva', 'Mysis larva', 'Polychaete larva', 'Coroniderichthus larva', 'Copepod nauplii & copepodids', 'Veliger larva', 'Zaitzevia larva', 'Sialis larva', 'Phyllosoma larva', and the 1.7 mg dry 'Ranzania laevis' of the Ikeda database, an ocean sunfish larva (adults weigh tens of kilograms): non-adult records (the life-stage rule of `audit/raw_name_patterns.csv`, applied here because the stage is written in the epithet position); (2) 111 rows (11 species) of Insecta: every insect of the file was incubated in water, so the species-level rows (*Chironomus plumosus*, *Chaoborus punctipennis*, *Tanypus chinensis*, *Tokunagayusurika akamusi*, *Campsurus melanocephalus*, *Drunella*, *Epeorus deceptivus*, *Amphicosmoecus canax*, ...) are larvae and nymphs, as the freshwater Odonata / Trichoptera / Diptera rows of Gonzalez_2025: non-adult records; (3) 417 rows (8 species) of Amphibia: tadpoles (Rugenski 2013: *Smilisca sila* at 5.6 mg dry, *Hyla colymba*, *Lithobates warszewitschii*; Vanni et al. 2002: *Bufo marinus* at 7.5 mg, *Rana palmipes*; Whiles et al. 2009) and larval or small salamanders (*Eurycea cirrigera* of Milanovich & Hopton 2014 at 0.2 g wet against 14.8 g in the database, *Desmognathus quadramaculatus* 0.4 g against 17 g, *Dicamptodon tenebrosus* of Munshaw et al. 2013 at 13 g): non-adult records, as the StoichLife amphibians (Gonzalez_2025). The fishes (3,642 rows of 137 species, 81 of them new to the database) were excluded in the first build (option b of issue #99) and are kept by owner decision of 2026-10-05 (option a). 7,340 rows kept. The 'Follum & Gray 1987' key names a bivalve paper (*Nucula tenuis*) but its 82 rows are *Nucula tenuis* (40 rows) and the crayfish *Faxonius propinquus* (42; the StoichLife copy of one such row was kept as miskeyed upstream, owner decision 2026-10-04), kept as the data have them. 'Gauvin et al 1989' (15 rows of *Gammarus lacustris*) and 'Nalepa et al 1991' (53 rows of *Lampsilis radiata*) are keys the Metadata S1 list does not print.

Source label: `Vanni_2017` (with the conversion CiteIDs above).
