# GATEWAy (Brose 2018) global food-web database body masses

Source: Brose, U. (2018) GlobAL daTabasE of traits and food Web Architecture (GATEWAy) version 1.0. iDiv Data Repository dataset 283, version 3 (2018-12-11). https://idata.idiv.de/ddm/Data/ShowData/283 (DataCite DOI 10.25829/idiv.283-3-756). The database is described in Brose, U. et al. (2019) Predator traits determine food-web architecture across ecosystems. Nature Ecology & Evolution 3:919-927, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-019-0899-x, whose Supplementary Table 1 lists the 290 food webs with their references (the SI is a publisher file and is not stored here).
Data: `283_2_FoodWebDataBase_2018_12_10.csv` (222,151 trophic-interaction rows, each with consumer and resource traits), `283_2_FoodWebDatabase_metadata.doc` and the iDiv metadata files; downloaded 2026-08-19.

What a GATEWAy body mass is: the metadata defines `con.mass.mean[g]` / `res.mass.mean[g]` as the "mean mass of the population that is involved in this trophic interaction - this can be all individuals of a species or sub-groups such as adults", in grams, and the 2019 paper states that all body masses are gram fresh masses. The 290 webs (`foodweb.name`) come from 23 studies (`link.citation`) whose contributors measured, estimated or compiled masses in their own way (`con.size.method`: measurement, regression, published account; coded for 18 % of the rows). A GATEWAy body mass is therefore the mean mass of the individuals sampled in the web, in grams fresh mass, and the database uses these values as the source reports them (owner decision 2026-10-03, issue #29; other sources that provide individual-level masses are likewise reduced to a mean). Such means sit below adult masses for fish in lake, river and pool webs, so the range filter (`log10_range > 1`) may remove a species where another source holds an adult mass. Issue #14 audited the unit and the usability of every web (section "Per-web table" below).

Columns used: `foodweb.name`, `link.citation`, `con.taxonomy`, `con.lifestage`, `con.mass.mean(g)`, `con.metabolic.type`; `res.taxonomy`, `res.lifestage`, `res.mass.mean(g)`, `res.metabolic.type`. Consumer and resource rows are stacked as separate records (one row per trophic link, so a species' mass is repeated across its links and webs; RunMe Pass 1 reduces these to one within-source geometric mean per species).
Filters: (1) life stage: consumer and resource rows whose life stage is adult (any value starting with 'adult', e.g. 'adults', 'adults; juveniles'), female, male or unspecified are kept; juveniles, larvae, nymphs, nauplii, pupae and eggs are dropped (`AdultOrUnspecified()` in `R/library/foodweb_units.r`, since 2026-09-24); (2) positive masses only; 350,810 stacked rows pass (1) and (2). (3) Group-level placeholder values: within one `link.citation`, a mean mass carried by five or more species-level names is a default assigned to a group of taxa, not a measurement of any of them (0.01 g on 115 tide-pool species including the crabs Eriphia smithii, Grapsus adscensionis and Xantho pilipes; 4.01e-5 g on 37 Chesapeake Bay species including Cheirocratus sundevalli; 1.177e-3 g on all four Chesapeake Gammarus species; 0.0507, 5.048e-3 and 7.402e-3 g on 13, 19 and 11 Kongsfjorden species; 5.6 g on 14 Weddell Sea species). Every row carrying such a value is dropped as imputed (`DropPlaceholders()`; one `audit/imputed_rows.csv` entry per citation): 63,044 rows of 1,064 taxon names in ten citations (Mendonca et al. 2018: 24,832 rows / 416 names; Kroll: 14,347 / 189; Jacob et al. 2011: 9,513 / 162; Digel et al. 2014: 6,217 / 46; Sutherland & Havens: 2,002 / 18; Gray & Thompson: 1,859 / 21; Jacob et al. 2015: 1,772 / 77; Piechnik: 961 / 20; Cattin Blandenier: 803 / 26; Ekloef et al. 2013: 738 / 89). (4) Per-web actions from `foodweb_units.csv` (`ApplyUnitActions()`): three groups of webs have no usable sizes and are dropped with a logged reason: the 96 intertidal rock pools outside the Gulf of St Lawrence (Mendonca et al. 2018; the source paper reports no body sizes, the masses are 0.01 g defaults and the juveniles present in the pools: Carcinus maenas 0.13-0.18 g; 17,045 rows, 235 names), Skipwith Pond (Warren 1989; the masses are one generic length-mass regression, M = 0.50 L(cm)^2.64, applied to every taxon from 0.4 mm cladocerans to 24 mm Dytiscus, 10-20x too high for insects; 372 rows, 19 names) and the ten Taieri River tributaries (Townsend et al. 1998; no body-size source, inconsistent values; 1,448 rows, 40 names). The 28 Gulf of St Lawrence pools (webs PP*, SF*) and every other web are kept. 268,901 rows remain.
Mass type: wet mass in grams as reported for the kept webs of 19 studies (per-web median log10 ratios to the other sources of the database within about +-0.2 for the studies with many shared species; `foodweb_units.csv`). Four studies report dry mass in grams and are converted to wet grams with `ToWetMass(from = 'dry')` and the group factors of `R/library/mass_conversion.r`: McLaughlin et al. 2010 (Gearagh forest floor, Ireland: adult carabids sit at the dry:wet ratio of the other sources, Pterostichus melanarius 36 vs 155 mg), O'Gorman et al. 2012 (Hengill streams, Iceland: the chapter's mass currency is dry mass), Mulder & Elser 2009 (Dutch grassland soils: the Dutch soil programme reports dry body mass, Cohen & Mulder 2014; the earthworms, whose values already match the wet masses of other sources, are not converted) and Layer et al. 2010 (ten UK streams of the 20-streams pH study, invertebrates only: the same group's Broadstone data are "dry body masses" in Brose et al. 2005; the trout stay as wet mass; the Mill Stream web is kept with its fish). Groups are assigned per taxon from the enrichment phylum and class (`MassGroupFromRanks()`: fish classes -> fish 0.2415; Insecta and other hexapods -> insect 0.35; Mollusca -> mollusc 0.1972, bivalves, gastropods and chitons as WHOLE wet mass through the Brey 2010 shell ratios 0.44 / 0.40 / 0.40, the convention of Eklof_etal_2017 and McCoy_2008, except the shell-less gastropods (slugs of the Arionidae, Agriolimacidae and other slug families, sea slugs; `ShellLessGastropod()`), which take the tissue factor only; Annelida -> annelid 0.1769; Echinodermata -> echinoderm 0.2250; everything else -> invertebrate 0.2144; names that do not resolve take the ranks of their genus, else the source's metabolic type) and recorded in `foodweb_units_groups.csv` (350 taxa; `convert = FALSE` for the Layer trout and the Mulder lumbricids). 8,180 rows are converted (7,857 with Brey 2010 factors, 323 insects with Studier & Sevick 1992); `LabelWithConversion()` appends the conversion CiteIDs to their label.
Source labels: 'Brose_etal_2018' (unconverted rows), 'Brose_etal_2018; Brey_2010' and 'Brose_etal_2018; Studier_1992' (converted rows); RunMe Pass 1 groups all three under the label Brose_etal_2018.
Imputed rows: none flagged by the source itself; the group-level placeholder values (63,044 rows) and the three dropped web groups (18,865 rows) are removed with `DropImputed()` and listed in `audit/imputed_rows.csv` with the rule or the reason. Isolated impossible records that the rules do not reach (the Weddell Sea sipunculans Golfingia margaritacea at 6.3e-7 g and Phascolion strombi at 6.3e-7 g, the Mill Stream Squalius cephalus at 17.4 kg and Gobio gobio at 1.23 kg, among others) are not corrected in the parser; they are listed in PR #14 for the lab Google Sheet override (`BM_data`), which replaces every compiled record of a taxon.
Name corrections: four one-token names of the raw file are misspelt genera and are renamed by `R/library/fix_misspellings.r` before RunMe files them as genus-level records (issue #47; GBIF `species/match`, rank genus): 'Heremodromia' (the five UK stream webs of Gray et al. 2015 / Thompson et al. 2017, 105 rows) -> Hemerodromia (Empididae), 'Telonemus' (Florida mangrove-island webs, 47 rows) -> Telenomus (Scelionidae), 'Tetraluerodes' (Florida islands, `taxonomy.level` genus, `common` 'whiteflies', 44 rows) -> Tetraleurodes (Aleyrodidae) and 'renic' (Carpinteria web, 24 adult rows; the Hechinger et al. 2011 node of that name, family Renicolidae, sized 'equal to congener' Renicola cerithidicola) -> Renicola (Renicolidae). Being genus-level records, these do not touch `TaxonBodyMass.csv`.

## Per-web table

`foodweb_units.csv` (tracked; one row per `foodweb.name`) is both the evidence record of the issue #14 audit and the instruction set the parser reads. Evidence columns: `n_rows` (resolved species-level rows of the web before the actions), `n_species`, `n_shared` (web x species pairs with another source), `median_log10_ratio` and `iqr_log10_ratio` (web geometric mean over the median Pass-1 value of the other sources), `unit_class` (g_wet, dry_or_afdw, mixed, undetermined), `proposed_action` (the Phase A proposal), `factor`, `evidence`, `refs`. Decision columns (owner decisions of 2026-10-03, recorded in the issue): `action` (keep | drop | convert_dry), `mass_group` (the rule, for convert_dry) and `log_reason` (the `DropImputed()` reason, for drop). The table is regenerated by `Rscript sources/databases/Brose_etal_2018/foodweb_units_analysis.r` from the repository root (needs a complete `sources/Rdata/` and `sources/enrich_cache.Rdata`), which merges the hand-made `foodweb_units_decisions.csv` (one row per citation; a row with a `foodweb.name` overrides its citation, used for the Canadian pools) into the computed evidence and writes `foodweb_units_groups.csv`; `tmp/foodweb_units_species.csv` holds the web x species detail. Shared code: `R/library/foodweb_units.r` (parser side) and `R/library/unit_audit.r` (analysis side).
