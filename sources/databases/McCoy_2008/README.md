# McCoy & Gillooly (2008) natural mortality data set: body masses of Appendix S1

Source: McCoy, M. W., & Gillooly, J. F. (2008) Predicting natural mortality rates of plants and animals. Ecology Letters 11:710-716. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1461-0248.2008.01190.x (erratum: Ecology Letters 12:731-733, https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1461-0248.2009.01338.x)
Data: Wiley supplementary file `ELE_1190_sm_AppendixS1` (Electronic Supplement 1, 'Temperature, Body Mass, and Mortality data with Sources', 68 pages: the table Group | Species | Dry mass (g) | Temp. C | *Mortality (y-1) | Ref. on pages 1-66, followed by a footnote on Fregata magnificens and the 29 numbered data references on pages 66-68), https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/action/downloadSupplement?doi=10.1111%2Fj.1461-0248.2008.01190.x&file=ELE_1190_sm_AppendixS1.doc, downloaded by M. Novak 2026-10-02 and exported to PDF (`tmp/ele_1190_sm_appendixs1.pdf`, git-ignored). The header note states "Dry mass was calculated as 1/4*wet mass." Brown, Hall & Sibly (2018) Nature Ecology & Evolution 2:262-268, Supplementary Table 1 (`../Brown_etal_2018/41559_2017_430_MOESM3_ESM.csv`) reproduces this appendix row for row without the reference column; the table was read from the Brown_etal_2018 folder from 2023-01-12 to 2026-10-02 and is now read from the appendix itself, whose per-row reference code decides the mass unit (issue #7).
Licence: publisher supplementary file (Wiley), not redistributed; only the parsed facts (`McCoy_2008_appendixS1.csv`), the parser and the reference list are committed. Re-create the CSV with `python3 parse_mcg_appendixS1.py [path to the PDF]` (needs `pdftotext` from poppler).

Parsing: `parse_mcg_appendixS1.py` runs `pdftotext -layout` and reads the table pages line by line. Every table row has one line carrying the dry mass in E-notation; wrapped cells are printed on text-only lines centred on that line (a two-line species name gives genus / numbers / epithet, the two-line group label gives 'Multicellular' / numbers / 'plant', a three-line cell puts its middle line on the number line), so the parser gives each number line the k text-only lines before it that the previous row did not claim and the same k after it, joins their group-column and species-column text, and assigns the numbers right of the mass to the Temp / Mortality / Ref columns by horizontal position (column centres per page), cross-checked against the print format (mortality has three decimals, temperatures at most two, references are comma lists of integers; a reference glued to the mortality value is split off after the third decimal). Output `McCoy_2008_appendixS1.csv`: 2,117 rows (Bird 779, Fish 234, Invertebrate 198, Mammal 524, Multicellular plant 348, Phytoplankton 34) with `group`, `species_printed` (verbatim, whitespace normalised), `dry_mass_g`, `temp_C`, `mortality_y`, `ref` (as printed) and `page`. Blank cells in the source: 36 temperatures (35 plant rows and one 'Mediterranean phytoplankton' row), 2 mortality rates ('Faramea juveniles', one 'Posidonica oceanica' row), 1 reference ('Parus inornatus'). No row failed to parse. The print carries a few artefacts kept verbatim in `species_printed` and handled downstream: 'Pseudopleuronecte s americanus' (word broken at the wrap), 'Strongylocentr. droeb.', 'Acipsnser fulvescens', 'Aechmophorus accidentalis', 'Sula (= Morus) bassanus', 'Lamellibranchia' (genus only, two rows), 'Catopsisssp.' and 'Arisae matriphyllum' (plants). The 29 data references (pages 66-68) are transcribed verbatim in `appendixS1_references.csv` (`ref`, `citation`, `doi`): 23 DOIs confirmed with Crossref on first author, year and journal; blank for 1 (Carey & Judge 2000, book), 2 (Dunning 1993, book), 9 (NOAA World Ocean Atlas 2001), 11 (Orr et al. 1998, NTIS report), 24 (Turner & Lutz 1984, Oceanus; no Crossref or Scite record) and 25 (Brey 2001, web handbook; URL in the citation).

Columns used: `group`, `species_printed`, `dry_mass_g`, `ref` (carried as `ref_keys`, commas replaced by '; ', e.g. '1; 2', '9; 10; 11').
Filters: Multicellular plant and Phytoplankton rows (382) are dropped first, unlogged (autotrophs). Fish and invertebrate rows are kept, used as printed or excluded per reference (see Mass type and Imputed rows). 'Nucella lapillus' (ref 25) is dropped because the appendix prints 2.060E+01 where Brown et al.'s copy has 2.06 and the two publications cannot be reconciled. 'Cathartes aura' (refs 1,2) is dropped because the appendix prints 3.668E+03 g 'dry', already twice the wet mass of a turkey vulture (1.4-2.0 kg; 12 other sources in this database): a decimal-exponent error that x4 would turn into 14.7 kg; the value is not corrected. Rows with a missing or non-positive mass would be dropped (none). Names: a parenthetical synonym ('Sula (= Morus) bassanus'), single-letter tokens (the abbreviated middle names of 'Accipiter n. nisus' and 'Cygnus c. columbianus', the 'f' of 'Bovallia gigantea f', the broken 's' of 'Pseudopleuronecte s americanus') and 'ssp.' / 'var. ...' suffixes are removed; the appendix's 'Strongylocentr.' / 'Strongylocentr' is expanded to Strongylocentrotus ('droeb.' -> droebachiensis; the printed genus is kept for S. franciscanus, the pipeline's synonym handling takes care of Mesocentrotus); trinomials are truncated and 'sp.' / 'spp.' names ('Nemipterus sp.', 'Dendroica coronata sp.') dropped by the pipeline, and the remaining misspellings are corrected by `R/library/fix_misspellings.r`, whose rules were written for the Brown_etal_2018 copy of the table ('Pseudopleuronecte_americanus', 'Aechmophorus_accidentalis', 'Aeronautes_sexatilis', ...).
Mass type: the 'Dry mass (g)' column is wet mass / 4 only where the reference supplied a wet mass; the unit follows the reference code, so the conversion is keyed on group x ref (owner decisions of 2026-10-02, issue #7; row counts are kept / excluded):
- Bird and Mammal, all refs (778 / 1 Cathartes aura, 524 / 0), and Fish refs 5, 4, 7, 8,9, 9,12, 9,13 and 9,14 (190 / 0): the references supplied wet masses (Carey & Judge 2000, Dunning 1993, Ricklefs 1998, Smith et al. 2003; Pauly's 1980 asymptotic weights, Gillooly et al. 2001, Childress et al. 1980, Hissmann et al. 1998, Smith et al. 1995, Kelly et al. 1997, Andrews et al. 1999), so `mass_g = value / 0.25` inverts the source's own ratio, with no external conversion reference: Accipiter gentilis 256.1 g x 4 = 1,024.4 g, the mean of the male and female masses in Dunning; within this database the ratio of other sources to these values is 3.999 for birds and 4.000 for mammals; Pauly's fish values are stock asymptotic weights (W-infinity) that after x4 sit at a median 0.34 of the FishBase maximum published weight (Hamel 2015 confirms the /4); Latimeria chalumnae 20 kg x 4 = 80 kg, the literature value; the table maximum 3.75e7 g x 4 = 1.5e8 g. 'Parus inornatus' (blank ref, refs 1,2 by context) is included.
- Fish, refs 9,10,11, Cailliet et al. (2001) and Orr et al. (1998) (40 / 0): the printed values are already wet maximum-type weights, not wet / 4, and are used as printed (x1): they sit at a median 0.39 of the FishBase maximum published weight (37 species with a maximum) and at 1.01x the other sources' adult masses, where Pauly's validated values reach 0.34 and 1.6 only after x4; x4 would exceed the FishBase maximum for 27 of the 37 species (Sebastes aleutianus 9.38 kg -> 37.5 kg; S. borealis 9.3 kg -> 37.2 kg against 16.2 kg).
- Fish, ref 6, Mauchline (1988) (0 / 4): Benthosema glaciale, Cyclothone braueri, Lampanyctus macdonaldi and Maurolicus muelleri fit neither reading (x4 is 10x the other sources' masses and exceeds the maximum size of Cyclothone braueri, 0.52 g against 0.44 g at the maximum length; x1 puts asymptotic weights at 0.03 of the maximum); the unit is undeterminable and the rows are excluded. Benthosema glaciale keeps its two Pauly (ref 5) rows.
- Invertebrate, ref 25, Brey (2001) data bank (116 / 1): the values are kJ per mean individual, not grams. Evidence: the temperatures end in .x5 (Kelvin - 273.15, the data bank's unit); for 25 species with independent adult masses the values exceed the documented maximum size of 5 species as grams and of 17 species as grams x 4, but of 1 (Nucella) as kJ, with median log10 errors against adult whole mass of +0.13 (as grams), +0.73 (x4) and -0.12 (kJ -> wet mass); soft-bodied taxa fit only as kJ (Chorismus antarcticus 12.7 -> 3.3 g against 2-3.4 g adults). Conversion with `ToWetMass(from = 'energy')` in `R/library/mass_conversion.r`: the value is divided by the class/order energy density, the species-level median of Brey et al. (2010) Conversion04 `J / mgWM` (= kJ per g wet mass; bivalve 3.0574, gastropod 3.9211, echinoid 1.1609, ophiuroid 2.0276, holothurian 1.7935, decapod 3.8962, amphipod 3.769, isopod 2.2817, polychaete 3.1341, aquatic insect 4.5745; the single polyplacophoran, Acanthochitona crinita, uses the pooled Mollusca 3.8817), and, because that density refers to shell-free tissue while molluscs enter this database as whole wet mass, the mollusc result is further divided by the Conversion04 shell ratio `WM / (WM+Shell)` (bivalves 0.44, gastropods 0.40; the chiton uses the gastropod ratio, the data bank having no Polyplacophora record): Spisula sachalinensis 181 / 3.057 / 0.44 = 134.5 g, Haliotis iris 363 / 3.921 / 0.40 = 231.4 g, Strongylocentrotus (Mesocentrotus) franciscanus 400 / 1.161 = 344.6 g and 648 / 1.161 = 558.2 g, Holothuria atra 880 / 1.794 = 490.7 g, Hediste diversicolor 1.22 / 3.134 = 0.39 g, Uca rapax 6.85 / 3.896 = 1.76 g. The class/order is assigned from a genus map in the script (72 genera; the taxonomy cache is not available at parse time), giving 54 bivalve, 13 gastropod, 1 polyplacophoran, 13 echinoid, 5 ophiuroid, 1 holothurian, 7 decapod, 6 amphipod, 2 isopod, 11 polychaete and 3 aquatic-insect rows. The conversion CiteID Brey_2010 is appended to the label.
- Invertebrate, ref 4, Gillooly et al. (2001) (16 / 43): Gammarus fossarum, Gammarus roeseli and Siliqua patula are wet / 4 and are kept as `value / 0.25`; the laboratory zooplankton (Daphnia, Acanthocyclops, Cyclops, Eucyclops, Mesocyclops, Keratella, Filinia, Notholca; 43 rows, 11 names) mix dry and dry/4 units and are excluded.
- Invertebrate, ref 6 alone, Mauchline (1988) (3 / 0): Boreomysis microps, Gnathophausia zoea and Gennadas elegans, `value / 0.25`.
- Invertebrate, refs 6,26, Mauchline euphausiids (0 / 6): units unverifiable, excluded.
- Invertebrate, deep-sea references (6 / 7): `value / 0.25` for ref 18 (Prochaetoderma yongei), 20 (Tindaria callistiformis), 22 Ophiocten hastatum, 23 (Echinus acutus, E. elegans) and 9,27 (Ophiocten gracilis); excluded for refs 15 and 16 (Lamellibranchia, genus only), 17 (Reticulammina labyrinthica, a xenophyophore), 19 (Vesicomyid sp.), 21 (Mytilid sp.), 24 and 22 Calyptogena magnifica, whose mass basis is undefined.

In total 1,673 of the 1,735 animal rows are kept (1,532 vertebrates: 778 birds, 230 fish, 524 mammals; 141 invertebrates, 116 of them energy-converted) and 62 excluded (60 logged; Nucella lapillus and Cathartes aura dropped outright). The whole-wet-mass convention for shelled taxa (shell ratios bivalve 0.44, gastropod 0.40, chiton and barnacle 0.40 by analogy) is shared with Eklof_etal_2017, whose tissue masses go through the same `ww_per_whole` step (`ToWetMass(whole = TRUE)`).
Source label: McCoy_2008 for the x4 and x1 rows (no conversion reference appended, as for Cohen_2014 and Mulder_2011, which also invert the source's own ratio); 'McCoy_2008; Brey_2010' for the 116 energy-converted rows.
Provenance: `ref_keys` holds the appendix's Ref code(s) per record, resolved in `appendixS1_references.csv`; 1,672 of the 1,673 kept records are keyed (Bird: '1; 2' 759, '3' 18, blank 1; Mammal: '1; 28' 498, '3' 26; Fish: '5' 175, '9; 10; 11' 40, '4' 6, '7' 5, '8; 9' 1, '9; 12' 1, '9; 13' 1, '9; 14' 1; Invertebrate: '25' 116, '4' 16, '6' 3, '23' 2, and one each of '18', '20', '22', '9; 27'). Reference 9 is the temperature atlas, 1 and 10 are longevity sources; the body-mass compilations are 2 (birds), 28 (mammals), 5 (fish asymptotic weights) and 25 (Brey's data bank, energy).
Imputed rows: none flagged in source. Excluded for unverifiable units with `DropImputed()` (logged to `audit/imputed_rows.csv`): 4 rows 'mesopelagic fish rows of undeterminable unit (ref 6)', 43 rows 'lab zooplankton rows of mixed dry/dry-quarter units (ref 4)', 6 rows 'euphausiid rows with unverifiable units (refs 6,26)', 7 rows 'deep-sea rows with undefined mass basis'.

Validation against Brown et al. (2018) Supplementary Table 1 (`tmp/validate_mcg_vs_moesm3.py`, 2026-10-02): a multiset join on group + genus + epithet + dry mass + temperature, with the same name cleaning on both sides, matches 2,021 of the 2,117 appendix rows to 2,021 of the 2,041 MOESM3 rows: all 779 bird and 524 mammal rows, 232 of 234 fish, 191 of 198 invertebrates, 295 of 348 plants and none of the 34 phytoplankton rows. Appendix rows without a counterpart: Fish 'Nemipterus sp.' (102 g, dropped by Brown) and 'Sebastes jorani' (152 g; MOESM3 spells it 'jordani', its only unmatched fish row); Invertebrate: the fifth 'Acanthocyclops robustus' row (25 C) and 'Ophiocten gracilis' 0.025 g (refs 9, 27), both absent from MOESM3; the two 'Lamellibranchia' rows (3.43 g; refs 15, 16), 'Mytilid sp.' 9.68 g and 'Vesicomyid sp.' 0.00238 g, which MOESM3 holds as malformed rows with the mass shifted into the Species column; and 'Nucella lapillus' 20.6 g against 2.06 g in MOESM3 at the same temperature and mortality, the only mass disagreement; 53 plant and 34 phytoplankton rows (MOESM3 has 12 malformed 'Quercuss' rows, one 'Catopsis' row and two phytoplankton rows of 1.9e-11 g with no counterpart in the appendix). Masses and temperatures agree for every matched row; 134 matched rows (Bird 1, Fish 58, Invertebrate 75) differ only in the mortality column, where MOESM3 gives the 'Uncorrected mortality rate' and the appendix its converted rate. An independent parse of the same PDF (second agent, `tmp/mcg_appendixS1_parsed.csv`) is identical on 2,114 of 2,117 rows; the three differences are the blank-cell rows ('Parus inornatus' mortality 0.323 without a reference; 'Faramea juveniles' and the 0.57 g 'Posidonica oceanica' row, reference 29 without a mortality), where the dump confirms this parse.
