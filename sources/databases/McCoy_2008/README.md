The Wiley supplementary PDF is a publisher file, not redistributed; only the parsed facts (`McCoy_2008_appendixS1.csv`), the parser and the reference list are committed. Re-create the CSV with `python3 parse_mcg_appendixS1.py [path to the PDF] [output CSV]` (needs `pdftotext` from poppler).

Parsing: `parse_mcg_appendixS1.py` runs `pdftotext -layout` and reads the table pages line by line. Every table row has one line carrying the dry mass in E-notation; wrapped cells are printed on text-only lines centred on that line (a two-line species name gives genus / numbers / epithet, the two-line group label gives 'Multicellular' / numbers / 'plant', a three-line cell puts its middle line on the number line), so the parser gives each number line the k text-only lines before it that the previous row did not claim and the same k after it, joins their group-column and species-column text, and assigns the numbers right of the mass to the Temp / Mortality / Ref columns by horizontal position (column centres per page), cross-checked against the print format (mortality has three decimals, temperatures at most two, references are comma lists of integers; a reference glued to the mortality value is split off after the third decimal). In the erratum PDF 28 wrapped rows straddle a page break, each with the first line of the wrapped cell printed on the number line at the foot of the page and the remaining line(s) at the top of the next ('Mammal Babyrousa 2.500E+04' / 'babyrussa'; 'Multicellular Acacia victoriae ...' / 'plant'; 'Phytoplankton Tropical Atlantic ...' / 'phytoplankton' / 'communities'), which the symmetric rule cannot see; after a page that ends in a number line the parser therefore tries giving 0, 1 or 2 leading lines of the next page to that row and searches the hypotheses across pages for the assembly in which no row is short of lines, none is left over, every row has a known group and a species of 1-4 words and the table ends complete. The 2008 PDF (no straddling rows) assembles under the same code with 0 extra lines everywhere and reproduces the previous CSV byte for byte. The parser also reads the numbered data references (joining wrapped lines, splitting the '9.' glued to the end of reference 8 and dropping two hyperlink-rendering artefacts of reference 25), bounds the reference codes by their count and compares each citation with `appendixS1_references.csv`, reporting differences on stderr. Output `McCoy_2008_appendixS1.csv`: 2,116 rows (Bird 779, Fish 234, Invertebrate 197, Mammal 524, Multicellular plant 348, Phytoplankton 34) with `group`, `species_printed` (verbatim, whitespace normalised), `dry_mass_g`, `temp_C`, `mortality_y`, `ref` (as printed) and `page`. Blank cells in the source: 36 temperatures (35 plant rows and one 'Mediterranean phytoplankton' row), 2 mortality rates ('Faramea juveniles', one 'Posidonica oceanica' row), 1 reference ('Parus inornatus'). No row failed to parse. The print carries a few artefacts kept verbatim in `species_printed` and handled downstream: 'Pseudopleuronecte s americanus' (word broken at the wrap), 'Strongylocentr. droeb.', 'Acipsnser fulvescens', 'Aechmophorus accidentalis', 'Sula (= Morus) bassanus', 'Lamellibranchia' (genus only), 'Catopsisssp.' and 'Arisae matriphyllum' (plants). The 30 data references (pages 66-68) are transcribed verbatim in `appendixS1_references.csv` (`ref`, `citation`, `doi`): 24 DOIs confirmed with Crossref on first author, year and journal (8, Froese & Palomares 2000, 10.1023/a:1007602613607, and 30, Taylor 1959, 10.1093/icesjms/25.1.93, also on volume and pages, 2026-10-04); blank for 1 (Carey & Judge 2000, book), 2 (Dunning 1993, book), 9 (NOAA World Ocean Atlas 2001), 11 (Orr et al. 1998, NTIS report), 24 (Turner & Lutz 1984, Oceanus; no Crossref or Scite record) and 25 (Brey 2001, web handbook; URL in the citation). Reference 8 is printed without the space before the journal ('chalumnaeEnvironmental Biology of Fishes') and reference 30 without a period after its number; both are kept as printed.

Columns used: `group`, `species_printed`, `dry_mass_g`, `ref` (carried as `ref_keys`, commas replaced by '; ', e.g. '1; 2', '9; 10; 11').
Filters: Multicellular plant and Phytoplankton rows (382) are dropped first, unlogged (autotrophs). Fish and invertebrate rows are kept, used as printed or excluded per reference (see Mass type and Imputed rows). 'Nucella lapillus' (ref 25) is dropped because the appendix prints 2.060E+01 where Brown et al.'s copy has 2.06 and the two publications cannot be reconciled. 'Cathartes aura' (refs 1,2) is dropped because the appendix prints 3.668E+03 g 'dry', already twice the wet mass of a turkey vulture (1.4-2.0 kg; 12 other sources in this database): a decimal-exponent error that x4 would turn into 14.7 kg; the value is not corrected (both values are printed unchanged in the erratum version). Rows with a missing or non-positive mass would be dropped (none). Names: a parenthetical synonym ('Sula (= Morus) bassanus'), single-letter tokens (the abbreviated middle names of 'Accipiter n. nisus' and 'Cygnus c. columbianus', the 'f' of 'Bovallia gigantea f', the broken 's' of 'Pseudopleuronecte s americanus') and 'ssp.' / 'var. ...' suffixes are removed; the appendix's 'Strongylocentr.' / 'Strongylocentr' is expanded to Strongylocentrotus ('droeb.' -> droebachiensis; the printed genus is kept for S. franciscanus, the pipeline's synonym handling takes care of Mesocentrotus); trinomials are truncated and 'sp.' / 'spp.' names ('Nemipterus sp.', 'Dendroica coronata sp.') dropped by the pipeline, and the remaining misspellings are corrected by `R/library/fix_misspellings.r`, whose rules were written for the Brown_etal_2018 copy of the table ('Pseudopleuronecte_americanus', 'Aechmophorus_accidentalis', 'Aeronautes_sexatilis', ...).
Mass type: the 'Dry mass (g)' column is wet mass / 4 only where the reference supplied a wet mass; the unit follows the reference code, so the conversion is keyed on group x ref (owner decisions of 2026-10-02, issue #7; row counts are kept / excluded):
- Bird and Mammal, all refs (778 / 1 Cathartes aura, 524 / 0), and Fish refs 5, 4, 7, 8,9, 9,12, 9,13 and 9,14 (190 / 0): the references supplied wet masses (Carey & Judge 2000, Dunning 1993, Ricklefs 1998, Smith et al. 2003; Pauly's 1980 asymptotic weights, Gillooly et al. 2001, Childress et al. 1980, Hissmann et al. 1998, Smith et al. 1995, Kelly et al. 1997, Andrews et al. 1999), so `mass_g = value / 0.25` inverts the source's own ratio, with no external conversion reference: Accipiter gentilis 256.1 g x 4 = 1,024.4 g, the mean of the male and female masses in Dunning; within this database the ratio of other sources to these values is 3.999 for birds and 4.000 for mammals; Pauly's fish values are stock asymptotic weights (W-infinity) that after x4 sit at a median 0.34 of the FishBase maximum published weight (Hamel 2015 confirms the /4); Latimeria chalumnae 20 kg x 4 = 80 kg, the literature value; the table maximum 3.75e7 g x 4 = 1.5e8 g. 'Parus inornatus' (blank ref, refs 1,2 by context) is included.
- Fish, refs 9,10,11, Cailliet et al. (2001) and Orr et al. (1998) (40 / 0): the printed values are already wet maximum-type weights, not wet / 4, and are used as printed (x1): they sit at a median 0.39 of the FishBase maximum published weight (37 species with a maximum) and at 1.01x the other sources' adult masses, where Pauly's validated values reach 0.34 and 1.6 only after x4; x4 would exceed the FishBase maximum for 27 of the 37 species (Sebastes aleutianus 9.38 kg -> 37.5 kg; S. borealis 9.3 kg -> 37.2 kg against 16.2 kg).
- Fish, ref 6, Mauchline (1988) (0 / 4): Benthosema glaciale, Cyclothone braueri, Lampanyctus macdonaldi and Maurolicus muelleri fit neither reading (x4 is 10x the other sources' masses and exceeds the maximum size of Cyclothone braueri, 0.52 g against 0.44 g at the maximum length; x1 puts asymptotic weights at 0.03 of the maximum); the unit is undeterminable and the rows are excluded. Benthosema glaciale keeps its two Pauly (ref 5) rows.
- Invertebrate, ref 25, Brey (2001) data bank (116 / 1): the values are kJ per mean individual, not grams. Evidence: the temperatures end in .x5 (Kelvin - 273.15, the data bank's unit); for 25 species with independent adult masses the values exceed the documented maximum size of 5 species as grams and of 17 species as grams x 4, but of 1 (Nucella) as kJ, with median log10 errors against adult whole mass of +0.13 (as grams), +0.73 (x4) and -0.12 (kJ -> wet mass); soft-bodied taxa fit only as kJ (Chorismus antarcticus 12.7 -> 3.3 g against 2-3.4 g adults). Conversion with `ToWetMass(from = 'energy')` in `R/library/mass_conversion.r`: the value is divided by the class/order energy density, the species-level median of Brey et al. (2010) Conversion04 `J / mgWM` (= kJ per g wet mass; bivalve 3.0574, gastropod 3.9211, echinoid 1.1609, ophiuroid 2.0276, holothurian 1.7935, decapod 3.8962, amphipod 3.769, isopod 2.2817, polychaete 3.1341, aquatic insect 4.5745; the single polyplacophoran, Acanthochitona crinita, uses the pooled Mollusca 3.8817), and, because that density refers to shell-free tissue while molluscs enter this database as whole wet mass, the mollusc result is further divided by the Conversion04 shell ratio `WM / (WM+Shell)` (bivalves 0.44, gastropods 0.40; the chiton uses the gastropod ratio, the data bank having no Polyplacophora record): Spisula sachalinensis 181 / 3.057 / 0.44 = 134.5 g, Haliotis iris 363 / 3.921 / 0.40 = 231.4 g, Strongylocentrotus (Mesocentrotus) franciscanus 400 / 1.161 = 344.6 g and 648 / 1.161 = 558.2 g, Holothuria atra 880 / 1.794 = 490.7 g, Hediste diversicolor 1.22 / 3.134 = 0.39 g, Uca rapax 6.85 / 3.896 = 1.76 g. In total 1,673 of the 1,734 animal rows are kept (1,532 vertebrates: 778 birds, 230 fish, 524 mammals; 141 invertebrates, 116 of them energy-converted) and 61 excluded (59 logged; Nucella lapillus and Cathartes aura dropped outright). The erratum leaves the kept records unchanged except for the respelled Sebastes jordani (152 g; GBIF had already matched the 2008 'Sebastes jorani' to Sebastes jordani, so only the taxon key and the name-change report change). The whole-wet-mass convention for shelled taxa (shell ratios bivalve 0.44, gastropod 0.40, chiton and barnacle 0.40 by analogy) is shared with Eklof_etal_2017, whose tissue masses go through the same `ww_per_whole` step (`ToWetMass(whole = TRUE)`).
