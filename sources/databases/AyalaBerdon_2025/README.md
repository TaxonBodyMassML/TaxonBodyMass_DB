# Ayala-Berdon et al. (2025) body masses of vespertilionid bats measured for basal metabolic rate

Source: Ayala-Berdon, J., Medina-Bello, K. I., Orozco-Lugo, L., Iniguez-Davalos, I., Guillen-Servent, A., & Martinez-Gomez, M. (2025) Linking digestive efficiency and climate to basal metabolic rate in bats of the family Vespertilionidae. Journal of Comparative Physiology B 195:577-587. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00360-025-01630-3 (published 2025-09-05; Crossref licence: Springer Nature text-and-data-mining terms, not an open licence). Bib key `Ayala-Berdon:2025aa` (`Citation.bib`).
Data: the Springer Electronic Supplementary Material `360_2025_1630_MOESM1_ESM.pdf` (13 pages, 'Supplementary material', https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00360-025-01630-3/MediaObjects/360_2025_1630_MOESM1_ESM.pdf), downloaded by M. Novak 2026-10-04; a publisher file under the article's Springer licence, kept locally and not committed. Its first table (pages 1-8) lists, for 39 species of Vespertilionidae, every basal-metabolic-rate measurement the study used: 'Bat species', 'Locality', 'Climate' (Cold / Warm), 'Mb (g)', 'BMR (mL O2 h-1)', 'Modeled D*m (%)' (apparent dry-matter digestibility computed from Mb with the Cabrera-Campos et al. 2021 equation) and 'References'. The table header says that Mb 'values were taken from bats measured previously to the metabolic rate estimations' for the authors' own bats and that for the bats 'retrieved from the bibliography, we used the Mb reported in the publication'; the abstract gives the range 3.4-30.3 g (the table's largest value is 27.0 g). A second table (pages 11-12, thermoneutral-zone limits of 10 species) carries no mass and is not used. The body-mass table was transcribed by hand from the page images into `360_2025_1630_MOESM1_ESM_table1.csv` (154 rows; columns `species`, `locality`, `climate`, `Mb_g`, `BMR_mlO2_h`, `Dm_modeled_pct`, `reference`, `page`, `row_note`), every cell as printed, a merged cell repeated on each of its rows. Transcription notes (`row_note`): the second *Nycticeius humeralis* row (11.0 g) has no reference cell of its own and is read as the continuation of the 'Genoud 1990' cell above it ('Genoud 1993' is centred on the two rows below, as for *Lasiurus seminolus*, whose Genoud 1990 and 1993 rows come in pairs); the *Myotis bechsteinii* row prints 'Germany' in the climate column (the locality, shifted one column); the *Myotis fortidens* Autlan row prints a modelled D*m of 4.4 (not a percentage; the column is not used); the species names 'Nyctalus geoffroyi' and 'Pipistrellus pipitrellus' are kept as printed (see Filters); two *Eptesicus fuscus* 'This study' rows (15.4 g / 3.3 mL O2 h-1 and 13.8 / 2.8) repeat an 'Ayala-Berdon et al. 2025' row of the same locality, and two *Rhogeessa tumida* Xalapa rows (3.5 / 10.7, 3.4 / 10.4) repeat *Rhogeessa alleni* rows, value for value; they are kept as printed (noted for the owner).

Columns used: `species` (the binomial as printed), `Mb_g`, `reference` (kept as `ref_keys`); `class` Mammalia and `order` Chiroptera are added as taxonomy hints (the source's family, Vespertilionidae, is not passed on because *Miniopterus schreibersii* is now placed in Miniopteridae).
Filters: all 154 rows are used (the script would drop a row without a positive numeric `Mb_g`, but none occurs). Two printed names are corrected by `R/library/fix_misspellings.r` (pipeline step 2): 'Nyctalus geoffroyi' -> *Nyctophilus geoffroyi* (the two papers the rows cite, Dixon & Rose 2003 and Hosken & Withers 1999, are studies of *Nyctophilus geoffroyi*, and no *Nyctalus geoffroyi* exists) and 'Pipistrellus pipitrellus' -> *Pipistrellus pipistrellus* (the cited Speakman et al. 1989 is on *P. pipistrellus*). The records are taken as adults: BMR is measured on resting, post-absorptive, non-reproductive adults by definition and the table carries no age or sex column; the main article (not on disk) was not checked.
Mass type: live ('wet') body mass in grams of the bat whose BMR was measured (the authors' own animals, weighed before the metabolic measurement) or the mass reported in the cited publication (a published mean, usually); no conversion. One row per measurement (`n = 1`): several rows per species, locality and study for the authors' own bats (individuals), one or two rows per literature study (seasonal or site means), so the species value is the geometric mean over rows of Pass 1 in RunMe.r. The mixture of individual measurements (124 rows of the authors' own bats under 'This study', 'Ayala-Berdon et al. 2025', 'Ayala-Berdon and Medina-Bello 2024' and 'Ayala-Berdon et al. unpublished data') and published means (30 rows, 21 species) is that of the source.
Imputed rows: none flagged in source and none dropped. The 'Modeled D*m' column is a modelled digestibility, not a mass, and is not read; every Mb value is a measurement or a published measured mass, including the 30 rows the authors label 'unpublished data' (their own measurements).
Source label: `AyalaBerdon_2025`.
Record set: 154 records of 39 printed names (39 species after the two corrections; 10 species measured by the authors, 29 taken from the literature, the paper's own counts); masses 3.4-27.0 g. Records per key: Ayala-Berdon et al. 2025 (47), This study (39), Ayala-Berdon et al. unpublished data (30), Ayala-Berdon and Medina-Bello 2024 (10), Genoud 1993 (6), Genoud 1990 (4), Becker et al. 2013 (2), Geiser and Brigham 2000 (2), one each for Baudinette et al. 2000, Currie et al. 2015, Dixon and Rose 2003, Hanus 1959, Hosken and Withers 1999, Kurta and Kunz 1988, Licht and Leitner 1967, McNab 1969, McNab 1989, Munoz-Garcia et al. 2012, Skara et al. 2021, Soras et al. 2022, Speakman et al. 1989, Speakman et al. 2003.
Provenance: compilation (`Bib/source_provenance_classes.csv`, default unknown); record-level, hop 1 through the `reference` key of every row (author-year as printed, 22 distinct keys), resolved in `references.csv` (columns `key`, `citation`: the key as printed in the table and the verbatim entry of the supplement's reference list, pages 8-10, 22 entries, typographic errors kept: 'Encarnaçao', '86-361-369', '(2022.', 'Pipistrettus', 'neutropical', 'long- eared'; the two keys 'This study' and 'Ayala-Berdon et al. unpublished data' stand for the authors' own measurements and are the compiler's `self` references). The key 'Speakman et al. 2003' is matched to the list's only 2003 entry, Speakman & Thomas (2003), a book chapter. The key 'McNab 1989' (*Myotis nigricans*, 3.7 g, 1 record) is in neither of the supplement's reference lists (which hold McNab 1969 and McNab 1980); by owner decision (2026-10-04) it is read as a printing error for McNab 1969 (the neotropical-bat study already cited by *Myotis austroriparius*): `references.csv` carries a `McNab 1989` row with the McNab 1969 entry, so the record resolves to the same DOI, and `primary_references.csv` notes the correction. Two list entries are cited by no row (Hosken and Withers 1997, *Chalinolobus gouldii*; McNab 1980). The second reference list (page 12-13, after the thermoneutral-zone table) repeats two entries of the first. Owner decisions (2026-10-04): Hanus 1959 and Speakman & Thomas 2003 approved as DOI-less entries (`nodoi`, fields from the raw text: Physiol Bohemoslov 8:250-259; chapter in Kunz & Fenton (eds) Bat Ecology, University of Chicago Press, pp 430-490); the duplicated *E. fuscus* and *R. tumida* rows stay as printed.
