# Hudson, Isaac & Reuman (2013) field metabolic rates and body masses of individual birds and mammals

Source: Hudson, L. N., Isaac, N. J. B., & Reuman, D. C. (2013) The relationship between body mass and field metabolic rate among individual birds and mammals. Journal of Animal Ecology 82:1009-1020. https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2656.12086 (open access; Europe PMC PMC3840704). Bib key `Hudson:2013aa` (`Citation.bib`).
Data: Wiley Supporting Information `jane12086-sup-0003-AppendixS5.csv` (Appendix S5, 'Database of individual FMR and body mass measurements'): 1,498 rows, columns `Class`, `Order`, `Family`, `Genus`, `Species`, `Study`, `M (kg)`, `FMR (kJ / day)`; 894 birds and 604 mammals of 133 species from 126 studies (the paper's own counts). Downloaded by M. Novak 2026-10-04 together with Appendix S1-S4 (`jane12086-sup-0001-appendixs1-s4-figs1-10-tables1-s4.pdf`, the methods supplement, a publisher file kept locally and not committed). The database holds every published doubly-labelled-water study the authors could find in which body mass was measured (not estimated) and mass and FMR were reported per individual (Appendix S1); an individual measured more than once enters once, as the mean of its measurements (Methods). Taxonomy follows Wilson & Reeder (2005) for mammals and Dickinson (2003) for birds, subspecies ignored; orangutan hybrids are entered as Pongo pygmaeus and Eulemur fulvus rufus x collaris hybrids as Eulemur fulvus (Appendix S1).
Licence: unknown; owner to check https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2656.12086 (the article is open access, Source line above, but the CC licence version is not on disk). `jane12086-sup-0003-AppendixS5.csv` is tracked; the Appendix S1-S4 PDF is a publisher file kept locally and not committed; `references.csv` is our transcription of Appendix S6. See sources/LICENSES.md.

Columns used: `Genus`, `Species` (joined to the binomial), `M (kg)`, `Study` (kept as `ref_keys`), `Class`, `Order`, `Family` (taxonomy hints). `FMR (kJ / day)` is not used.
Filters: rows of the four studies that measured nestlings or chicks are dropped (see Imputed rows); otherwise all rows are used (the script would drop a row without a positive numeric `M (kg)`, but none occurs). The remaining records are taken as adults: the database carries no life-stage column, Appendices S1-S4 and the Methods say nothing about age classes, and the dropped studies are the only ones whose Appendix S6 titles identify the individuals as young. Two kept studies leave a doubt for the owner: Pontzer et al. (2010) measured four orangutans of 25.5-116.6 kg (the smallest may be a juvenile) and Nagy et al. (1995) honey possums of 5.3-17.8 g (the lightest below the adult range Bradshaw & Bradshaw 1999 report for the same species, 6.2-14.4 g); both are kept because nothing on disk identifies them as non-adults.
Mass type: live ('wet') body mass in kg of the individual measured by doubly labelled water, converted to grams (x 1000); no other conversion. One row per individual (`n = 1`), several rows per species and study (populations and individuals), so the species value is the geometric mean over individuals of Pass 1 in RunMe.r. The individuals are free-living (or in field-like captivity) adults of both sexes in whatever state the FMR study sampled them (breeding, incubating, lactating, fasting), so a species value here is a field mean, not a handbook adult mass.
Imputed rows: none flagged in source. The 63 rows (4 species) of the nestling and chick studies Degen et al. (1992, Lanius excubitor, 2 rows), Dykstra & Karasov (1993, Troglodytes aedon, 17), Dykstra et al. (2001, Haliaeetus leucocephalus, 16) and Tjorve et al. (2007, Haematopus moquini, 28) are dropped through `DropImputed()` as non-adult records (logged to audit/imputed_rows.csv); no other study of these species is in the database, so the four species leave with them. 1,435 rows kept.
Source label: `Hudson_2013`.
Provenance: compilation (`Bib/source_provenance_classes.csv`, default unknown); record-level, hop 1 through the `Study` key of every row (author-year, 126 keys), resolved in `references.csv` (columns `key`, `citation`: the key as written in Appendix S5 and the verbatim entry of Appendix S6, 'Database references', 126 entries). Appendix S6 is the separate Wiley supplement; the transcription was made with pdftotext from the open-access copy of the supplement in Europe PMC (PMC3840704, file `jane0082-1009-sd3.pdf`), a publisher file kept locally and not committed. Appendix S1-S4's own reference list (22 entries) covers only the works cited in the appendix text, not the studies. Transcription notes: the S6 entries are kept verbatim with their typographic errors ('Galpagos', 'Austraila', 'listory', 'Sanson, G.D. & K, J.N.', 'Obst, Bryan, S.'); three keys differ from their S6 entry and were matched by hand: `Tjørve et al 2007` (S6 prints 2006; Zoology 110 is the 2007 volume), `Jonsson et al 1996` (S6: Jönsson) and `Nagy et al 1990a` / `Nagy et al 1990b`, which S6 lists as two unsuffixed 'Nagy ... (1990)' entries; a and b were assigned in S6's order (a = Nagy, Bradley & Morris, quokkas and tammars; b = Nagy, Sanson & K., macropods and a ruminant), which Appendix S5's species (a: Setonix brachyurus, Macropus eugenii) confirm. Hoehler et al. (2023) Dataset S01 copies these rows under Reference Code 'Hudson et al. (2013)' (1,497 rows: all 604 mammal rows identically, 631 of 893 bird rows; the other 262 bird masses sit under the wrong species in Dataset S01); since issue #66 (2026-10-05) the Hoehler parser drops all 1,497 copies (`audit/imputed_rows.csv`), `Bib/source_dependencies.csv` keeps `Hoehler_etal_2023 -> Hudson_2013` as a `provenance_only` edge and the Hoehler README explains it.
