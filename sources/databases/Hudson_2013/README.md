# Hudson, Isaac & Reuman (2013) field metabolic rates and body masses of individual birds and mammals

Source: Hudson, L. N., Isaac, N. J. B., & Reuman, D. C. (2013) The relationship between body mass and field metabolic rate among individual birds and mammals. Journal of Animal Ecology 82:1009-1020. https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2656.12086 (open access; Europe PMC PMC3840704). Bib key `Hudson:2013aa` (`Citation.bib`).
Data: Wiley Supporting Information `jane12086-sup-0003-AppendixS5.csv` (Appendix S5, 'Database of individual FMR and body mass measurements'): 1,498 rows, columns `Class`, `Order`, `Family`, `Genus`, `Species`, `Study`, `M (kg)`, `FMR (kJ / day)`; 894 birds and 604 mammals of 133 species from 126 studies (the paper's own counts). Downloaded by M. Novak 2026-10-04 together with Appendix S1-S4 (`jane12086-sup-0001-appendixs1-s4-figs1-10-tables1-s4.pdf`, the methods supplement) and Appendix S6 (the reference list of the studies, a separate supplement file; the open-access copy of Europe PMC, PMC3840704, file `jane0082-1009-sd3.pdf`, is the one read here) -- both publisher files kept locally and not committed. The database holds every published doubly-labelled-water study the authors could find in which body mass was measured (not estimated) and mass and FMR were reported per individual (Appendix S1); an individual measured more than once enters once, as the mean of its measurements (Methods). Taxonomy follows Wilson & Reeder (2005) for mammals and Dickinson (2003) for birds, subspecies ignored; orangutan hybrids are entered as Pongo pygmaeus and Eulemur fulvus rufus x collaris hybrids as Eulemur fulvus (Appendix S1).

See sources/LICENSES.md.

Columns used: `Genus`, `Species` (joined to the binomial), `M (kg)`, `Study` (kept as `ref_keys`), `Class`, `Order`, `Family` (taxonomy hints). `FMR (kJ / day)` is not used.
Filters: rows of the four studies that measured nestlings or chicks are dropped (see Imputed rows); otherwise all rows are used (the script would drop a row without a positive numeric `M (kg)`, but none occurs). The remaining records are taken as adults: the database carries no life-stage column, Appendices S1-S4 and the Methods say nothing about age classes, and the dropped studies are the only ones whose Appendix S6 titles identify the individuals as young. Two kept studies leave a doubt for the owner: Pontzer et al. (2010) measured four orangutans of 25.5-116.6 kg (the smallest may be a juvenile) and Nagy et al. (1995) honey possums of 5.3-17.8 g (the lightest below the adult range Bradshaw & Bradshaw 1999 report for the same species, 6.2-14.4 g); both are kept because nothing on disk identifies them as non-adults.
Mass type: live ('wet') body mass in kg of the individual measured by doubly labelled water, converted to grams (x 1000); no other conversion. One row per individual (`n = 1`), several rows per species and study (populations and individuals), so the species value is the geometric mean over individuals of Pass 1 in RunMe.r. The individuals are free-living (or in field-like captivity) adults of both sexes in whatever state the FMR study sampled them (breeding, incubating, lactating, fasting), so a species value here is a field mean, not a handbook adult mass.
Imputed rows: none flagged in source. The 63 rows (4 species) of the nestling and chick studies Degen et al. (1992, Lanius excubitor, 2 rows), Dykstra & Karasov (1993, Troglodytes aedon, 17), Dykstra et al. (2001, Haliaeetus leucocephalus, 16) and Tjorve et al. (2007, Haematopus moquini, 28) are dropped through `DropImputed()` as non-adult records (logged to audit/imputed_rows.csv); no other study of these species is in the database, so the four species leave with them. 1,435 rows kept.
Source label: `Hudson_2013`.
Provenance: compilation (`Bib/source_provenance_classes.csv`, default unknown); record-level, hop 1 through the `Study` key of every row (author-year, one key per row, 126 keys in the file, 122 on the 1,435 kept records; the four nestling studies' keys leave with their rows), resolved in `references.csv` (columns `key`, `citation`, `owner_review`): the 126 entries of Appendix S6 ('Database references', 8 pages) transcribed verbatim by `parse_hudson_s6.py` (PyMuPDF) from the supplement PDF, in S6's order, each keyed in the form Appendix S5 uses ('Ballance 1995', 'Costa & Gales 2000', 'Nagy et al 1990a'; the first surname with its particle, 'von Helversen & Reyer 1984'). The script derives the key from the entry's author block and year and corrects seven by an explicit table where S5 departs from S6's text: `Tjørve et al 2007` (S6 prints 2006; Zoology 110 is the 2007 volume), `Jonsson et al 1996` (S6: Jönsson), `Nagy et al 1990a` / `Nagy et al 1990b` (two unsuffixed 'Nagy ... (1990)' entries, a and b in S6's order, which Appendix S5's species -- a: Setonix brachyurus, Macropus eugenii -- confirm), `Nagy & Obst 1992` and `Utter & LeFebvre 1973` (a given name printed as a surname, 'Obst, Bryan, S.', 'LeFebvre, Eugene, A.') and `Williams & Du Plessis 1996` (S6: du Plessis); it exits non-zero unless every `Study` value of Appendix S5 resolves. Entries are kept as printed, typographic errors included ('Galpagos', 'Austraila', 'listory', 'Sanson, G.D. & K, J.N.'); the Computer Modern ligatures are expanded and the accents dvips prints as separate glyphs are composed with their letter ('Bührmann', 'Adélie', 'Lindström, Ä.' as printed). The list's style, 'Authors (year) Title, Journal, volume, pages.', is parsed by `ParseCommaStyle()` (`R/library/citations/normalise_citation.r`); the two book chapters (Drack et al. 1999, Fleming 1988) and the proceedings paper (Nagy et al. 1999) carry `owner_review` and are queued for the owner whatever the services say. Appendix S1-S4's own reference list (22 entries) covers only the works cited in the appendix text, not the studies.
